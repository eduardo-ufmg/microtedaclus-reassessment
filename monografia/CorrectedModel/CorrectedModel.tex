\section{Order-Dependent Characteristics}
\label{sec:order_dependent_characteristics}

An intuitive approach to solve the order-dependence of the microcluster density is to redefine it as an aggregation of the Typicality and Eccentricity Data Analytics variables across all aggregated samples. We show, however, that such solution is analytically flawed.

If

\begin{equation}
    V_k = \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2
\end{equation}

is the sum of squared Euclidean deviations for the $S_k$ samples of a microcluster $i$, for the standard spatial sample variance $\sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} V_k$,

\begin{equation}
    V_k = S_k \sigma^2_k
\end{equation}

Then, $\bar{\xi}_k$, the mean eccentricity of a microcluster $k$, is defined as \cref{eq:mean_eccentricity}.

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{1}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{S_k \sigma^2_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) = \frac{2}{S_k}
    \label{eq:mean_eccentricity}
\end{align}

If the exact recursive formulas from the original \MicroTEDAClus formulation are injected instead \cite{maia2020evolvclus}, the cancellation persists. The original formulation incorporates an anomalous penalty by the dimensionality $d$ squared in its variance computation, such that

\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{4\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{d^2}
\end{equation}

Which corresponts to $V_k = \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4}$. The original eccentricity formula is \cref{eq:original_eccentricity}.

\begin{equation}
    \xi(x_j) = \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d}
    \label{eq:original_eccentricity}
\end{equation}

Thus,

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2 V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \left( \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4} \right) \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{d}{2} \right)
\end{align}

In both cases, the spatial dispersion term $\sigma^2_k$ cancels out entirely. Because the geometric volume is analytically eliminated, the aggregated TEDA variables reduce strictly to a function of cardinality $S_k$ and dimensionality $d$. Consequently, any density metric derived from them collapses into a pure mass count, rendering the algorithm mathematically blind to actual spatial dispersion.

To formulate a geometrically valid and order-invariant density, the microcluster characterization is partitioned into intrinsic state variables and transient relational metrics. The TEDA variables $\xi(x)$, $\zeta(x)$, $\tau(x)$, and $t(x)$ are strictly preserved as point-to-set transient functions evaluated for an incoming sample $x$ against the distribution \cite{angelov2014outside}. They are explicitly removed from the microcluster's intrinsic state variables.

The microcluster state is thus restricted to its recursively updated sufficient statistics: $S_k$, $\mu_k$, and $\sigma^2_k$. Because the model characterizes the spatial dispersion of a $d$-dimensional microcluster using a single scalar variance $\sigma^2_k$ rather than a full covariance matrix, it mathematically enforces an isotropic geometric assumption. Under this assumption, the true spatial volume scales with $(\sigma^2_k)^{d/2}$. To avoid the numerical instability of exponential dimensionality scaling in continuous stream clustering, a linear proxy of variance is utilized as a standard algebraic relaxation.

However, evaluating density directly as a ratio of cardinality to variance exposes a fundamental statistical constraint during the cluster initialization phase. In classical statistical estimation, sample variance possesses $S_k - 1$ degrees of freedom; for a single discrete observation, spatial dispersion is mathematically undefined. For a pair of points, the variance reflects only an unrepresentative collinear displacement, and the TEDA dynamic outlier boundary $m(k)$ does not commence until $S_k \ge 3$. Evaluating an uninitialized singleton unit with a standard denominator stabilization parameter $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ naively yields an artificial numerical singularity $D_k = 1/\epsilon = 10^8$. In a streaming environment, assigning a newborn candidate unit a density millions of times larger than well-established clusters is physically ungrounded.

In accordance with the baseline implementation, the order-independent microcluster density $D_k$ is formally defined as a piecewise function in \cref{eq:corrected_density}.

\begin{equation}
    D_k = \begin{cases}
        0, & S_k < 3 \\
        \dfrac{S_k}{\sigma^2_k + \epsilon}, & S_k \ge 3
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:corrected_density}
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon > 0$ serves strictly as a numerical regularization constant guarding against division-by-zero for collinear or identical samples in mature clusters. By defining $D_k = 0$ for $S_k < 3$ directly at the microcluster level, the state invariants remain strictly bounded, eliminating the unphysical $1/\epsilon$ singularity at its source.

Within macroclusters, the activation threshold filters out low-density boundary elements by comparing each microcluster's density against the concept's mean density. To ensure that incubating units undergoing statistical accumulation do not dilute or distort this threshold, the macrocluster mean density is evaluated exclusively across mature microclusters:

\begin{equation}
    \bar{D}_k^m = \begin{cases}
        \dfrac{1}{|M_k^{m, \text{mature}}|} \displaystyle\sum_{i \in M_k^{m, \text{mature}}} D_k^i, & \text{if } |M_k^{m, \text{mature}}| > 0 \\
        0, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:mature_mean_density}
\end{equation}

where $M_k^{m, \text{mature}} = \{i \in M_k^m \mid S_k^i \ge 3\}$. A microcluster $i \in M_k^m$ is subsequently designated as active if and only if it is mature and exceeds the macrocluster average:

\begin{equation}
    \text{active}_k^i = (S_k^i \ge 3) \land (D_k^i \ge \bar{D}_k^m)
    \label{eq:microcluster_activation}
\end{equation}

Microclusters failing this criterion are excluded from the active graph $\mathbb{G}_1$. This formulation resolves the order-dependent bias of the original model while insulating the active concept boundaries from newborn initialization artifacts.

This correction results in a slight decrease in clustering quality, as shown by \cref{tab:orderdep_ari_overall,tab:orderdep_ari_fifth,tab:orderdep_ari_steadystate,tab:orderdep_ari_diff}. We argue that such sacrifice is justified by the analytical correctness improvement, which is expected to support the following corrections and improvements of the model.

\section{Dimensionality Penalty}
\label{sec:dimensionality_penalty}

In the original formulation \cite{maia2020evolvclus}, the spatial dispersion metrics explicitly penalize the Euclidean norm by the data dimensionality $d$, as discussed in \cref{subsubsec:dimensionality_penalty}. Specifically, the recursive variance $\left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2$ and the point-to-set eccentricity $\xi^i(x_k)$ arbitrarily scale the squared deviations by $4/d^2$ and $2/d$, respectively. This scaling factor is analytically flawed. It distorts the true geometric boundaries of the microclusters and deviates from the standard TEDA derivations for the Euclidean space \cite{angelov2014outside}, where the spatial variance strictly reflects the unscaled trace of the covariance matrix.

To restore the analytical integrity of the spatial boundaries, the dimensionality penalty is removed. The recursive variance update for a microcluster $i$ at moment $k$ is corrected to the standard formulation:

$$ \left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2 = \frac{S^i_k - 1}{S^i_k} \left(\sigma^i_{k-1}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{S^i_k - 1} \lVert x_k - \mu^i_k \rVert^2 $$

Correspondingly, the transient point-to-set eccentricity evaluation is restored to its exact unscaled geometric form:

$$ \xi^i(x_k) = \frac{1}{S^i_k} + \frac{\lVert \mu^i_k - x_k \rVert^2}{S^i_k \left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2} $$

\Cref{tab:dimpen_ari_overall,tab:dimpen_ari_fifth,tab:dimpen_ari_steadystate,tab:dimpen_ari_diff} show that the removal of the dimensionality penalty had significant positive impact on clustering quality for the higher dimensional datasets \datasetname{SCL-32} and \datasetname{SCL-64}, while having negligible effect on the lower dimensional datasets. It indicates that the original dimensionality penalty was a significant source of distortion for the higher dimensional datasets, and its removal restores the true geometric boundaries of the microclusters.

\section{Correction of the Typicality Formulation}
\label{sec:correction_typicality}

The evaluation of the original implementation reveals a critical mathematical discrepancy in the computation of a sample's membership to a macrocluster. The algorithm assigns a sample $x_k$ to a macrocluster by weighting the typicality of the sample against the active microclusters. However, the implemented typicality formulation evaluates an unnormalized intermediate expression, equivalent to $1 - \zeta^i(x_k)$. This expression contradicts the foundational definitions of the TEDA framework \cite{angelov2014outside}. In the formal mathematical model, absolute typicality is strictly defined as $\tau^i(x_k) = 1 - \xi^i(x_k)$. Furthermore, to construct valid density-based membership probabilities, the aggregation must utilize normalized typicality, defined as:

\begin{equation}
    t^i(x_k) = \frac{\tau^i(x_k)}{S^i_k - 2}
\end{equation}

By omitting the cardinality normalization factor $S^i_k - 2$, the original algorithm fails to appropriately scale the typicality across microclusters of varying sizes, inherently invalidating the resulting membership distributions. To restore theoretical consistency, the membership calculation is corrected to explicitly compute the exact normalized typicality $t^i(x_k)$. It is important to note that this normalized formulation is mathematically defined exclusively for microclusters containing more than two samples. This domain constraint is naturally satisfied in the execution pipeline; because the macrocluster activation condition strictly filters out microclusters with $S^i_k \le 2$ prior to the membership evaluation, the denominator is mathematically guaranteed to be strictly positive.

Overall and initial quality are shown in \cref{tab:typicality_ari_overall,tab:typicality_ari_fifth} to be slightly worsened by the correction of the typicality formulation. However, \cref{tab:typicality_ari_steadystate,tab:typicality_ari_diff} show an also slight improvement in steadystate quality, indicating that the correction of the typicality formulation has a positive effect on the long-term stability of the clustering process.

\section{Inconsistent Decayment Logic}
\label{sec:inconsistent_decayment_logic}

While the original formulation in \cite{maia2020evolvclus} defined no temporal decay mechanism, the accompanying implementation incorporates an explicit fading factor $\rho = \frac{1}{\text{decay}}$ operating across two distinct routines (\cref{snippet:maia_implementation_prune_microclusters,snippet:maia_implementation_update_life}). This implemented mechanism diverges from standard literature formulations in two fundamental respects:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Heuristic Spatial Distance Ratio}: In \codename{update\_life}, whenever an incoming sample $x_k$ is evaluated against a microcluster $i$ with variance $\sigma_k^2 > 0$, its vitality $v_k^i$ is updated via an ad-hoc distance ratio:
    \begin{equation}
        v_k^i = v_{k-1}^i + \left(\frac{r_k^i - \lVert x_k - \mu_k^i \rVert}{r_k^i}\right) \rho
        \label{eq:heuristic_distance_decay}
    \end{equation}
    where If the incoming point falls within the spatial radius, vitality is rewarded; otherwise, it is penalized proportionally to the distance deficit. This spatial ratio lacks theoretical derivation in both \MicroTEDAClus \cite{maia2020evolvclus} and the foundational TEDA \cite{angelov2014outside}.
    \item \textbf{Coupling Pruning to Macrocluster Activation}: In \codename{prune\_micro\_clusters}, vitality is decremented by $\rho$ strictly if the microcluster is marked as inactive:
    \begin{equation}
        v_k^i = v_{k-1}^i - \rho \quad \text{if } \neg \text{active}_k^i
        \label{eq:activation_coupled_pruning}
    \end{equation}
    and any microcluster satisfying $v_k^i < 0$ is pruned from memory.
\end{enumerate}

This logic directly contradicts the theoretical description provided in the doctoral thesis presenting the updated \MicroTEDAClus \cite{galindo2021lastmile}. While the text of \cite{galindo2021lastmile} specifies that temporal decay should be applied to microclusters that are \textit{not updated} by the current sample, the implemented pruning routine couples decay exclusively to macrocluster activation status. As formalized in \cref{sec:order_dependent_characteristics}, activation is governed by sample maturity and relative density. Consequently, in the reference implementation, active microclusters never experience temporal decay in \codename{prune\_micro\_clusters}, while inactive microclusters decay unconditionally regardless of whether an incoming sample is proximate.

To restore methodological consistency with the conceptual description in \cite{galindo2021lastmile}, we first formulated a principled decay model. This formulation establishes three formal properties:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Outlier Inertia Decay}: Whenever an incoming sample $x_k$ is classified as an outlier by a microcluster $i$ according to the dynamic TEDA boundary, the microcluster vitality is decremented inversely proportional to its accumulated sample inertia $S_k^i$:
    \begin{equation}
        v_k^i = v_{k-1}^i - \frac{\rho}{S_k^i}, \quad \rho = \frac{1}{\text{decay}}
        \label{eq:outlier_inertia_decay}
    \end{equation}
    The scaling factor $\frac{1}{S_k^i}$ reflects the statistical mass of the cluster, granting established units greater resilience against transient noise.
    \item \textbf{Inlier Vitality Refresh}: Whenever an incoming sample is absorbed as an inlier, the microcluster vitality is refreshed to full capacity:
    \begin{equation}
        v_k^i = 1.0
        \label{eq:inlier_vitality_refresh}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Structural Pruning}: Pruning is entirely decoupled from the macrocluster graph and density activation. At each step, any microcluster whose vitality drops to zero is pruned immediately:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Prune } i \iff v_k^i \le 0
        \label{eq:decoupled_prune_rule}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Although the literature-derived \modelname{decay} formulation is theoretically elegant, preliminary experimental evaluation revealed two severe structural pathologies that rendered the model unviable in streaming environments:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Immortality Problem}: Under \cref{eq:outlier_inertia_decay}, as a microcluster matures and accumulates samples, the per-outlier decay step $\Delta v_k^i = \frac{\rho}{S_k^i}$ asymptotically vanishes. For instance, for a mature unit with $S_k^i = 500$ and a typical hyperparameter $\text{decay} = 100$, each outlier presentation reduces vitality by only $\Delta v = \frac{0.01}{500} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$. To be pruned from memory, the unit would need to observe $50000$ consecutive outliers without absorbing a single inlier. In practice, mature microclusters become immortal, remaining in memory indefinitely even after the underlying data distribution has drifted completely away.
    \item \textbf{Microcluster Explosion and Quadratic Slowdown}: Because obsolete and drifting microclusters were never reclaimed, the total microcluster count $|\mathbb{M}|$ accumulated monotonically. On complex benchmark datasets such as \ChameleonDSThree, microcluster counts in preliminary executions escalated from approximately $120$ units to over $4000$ units. Because macrocluster construction and boundary evaluation scale quadratically due to pairwise intersection checks, this structural accumulation induced a staggering $16\times$ computational slowdown, violating the real-time processing requirements of data streams.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve the immortality problem while preserving inertia-based resilience for nascent candidate units, we introduce a bounded decay formulation. In it, the inertia scaling factor is saturated at a strict upper threshold $S_{\max} = 10$:
\begin{equation}
    v_k^i = v_{k-1}^i - \frac{\rho}{\min(S_k^i, S_{\max})}, \quad \text{with } S_{\max} = 10
    \label{eq:bounded_inertia_decay}
\end{equation}

For candidate microclusters undergoing initial parameter estimation, the penalty scales with $S_k^i$, providing an incubation window to aggregate local points. For mature units, the per-outlier penalty is strictly lower-bounded by $\frac{\rho}{10} = \frac{1}{10 \cdot \text{decay}}$. This mathematical guarantee ensures that any mature microcluster devoid of incoming inliers is definitively evicted after at most $10 \times \text{decay}$ consecutive outlier presentations, precluding the immortality pathology.

Furthermore, we refined the hyperparameter search space. In the unconstrained search space, Optuna could select extreme values that caused either aggressive premature cluster destruction or effectively disabled pruning.

\section{Macrocluster Topology and Graph Processing}
\label{sec:macrocluster_topology}

The original \MicroTEDAClus formulation defines macroclusters as the connected components of the graph induced by the intersection condition of \cref{eq:microcluster_intersection_condition}, and restricts the macrocluster's calculations to the microclusters that satisfy the activation criterion of \cref{critique:use_of_unstable_density} \cite{maia2020evolvclus}. The reference implementation, as described in \cref{alg:microcluster_activation_criterion_pseudocode}, executes a two-pass procedure instead: it builds the intersection graph $\mathbb{G}_0$, evaluates the activation of each microcluster within its component, removes the non-active microclusters to obtain the active graph $\mathbb{G}_1$, and finally takes the connected components of $\mathbb{G}_1$ as the macroclusters. Our reassessment regarded this second pass as an undocumented deviation that adds graph-processing cost. The correction initially planned for this stage was therefore to maintain $\mathbb{G}_0$ as the single macrocluster topology and to apply the activation criterion exclusively as a filter over the terms of \cref{eq:macrocluster_typicality}, at prediction time.

The outcome of \cref{sec:inconsistent_decayment_logic} requires this plan to be reevaluated before it is implemented, for two reasons. First, the plan presumes that activation is a transient quantity, required only when a sample's membership is computed. The retention of \cref{eq:activation_coupled_pruning} establishes the opposite: activation is a persistent state variable that governs the survival of every microcluster. It must be evaluated for each component of $\mathbb{G}_0$ at every macrocluster update, regardless of how the labels are subsequently extracted, so the planned correction cannot eliminate the activation pass. It can only suppress the duplication of the graph and the second extraction of connected components. Second, the decay stage sets a methodological precedent. A mechanism of the reference implementation that lacks derivation in the published formulation was replaced by its literature-consistent counterpart, and the replacement degraded the model. The two-pass topology is a mechanism of the same nature, and it is built upon the same activation state. Its critique must thus be tested rather than assumed.

Let $A_k = \{i \mid \text{active}_k^i\}$ be the set of active microclusters according to \cref{eq:microcluster_activation}. The active graph is the subgraph of $\mathbb{G}_0$ induced by the active microclusters,

\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{G}_1 = \mathbb{G}_0\left[A_k\right]
    \label{eq:active_graph_induced}
\end{equation}

and, under the planned single-graph formulation, the typicality of a sample $x_k$ to a component $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathbb{G}_0$ would be

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(x_k) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C} \cap A_k} \frac{D^i_k}{\sum_{l \in \mathcal{C} \cap A_k} D^l_k} \, t^i(x_k)
    \label{eq:single_graph_typicality}
\end{equation}

Three properties follow directly from \cref{eq:active_graph_induced,eq:single_graph_typicality}.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Topologies Differ Only Through Inactive Bridges}: Every component of $\mathbb{G}_1$ is contained in exactly one component of $\mathbb{G}_0$, and both formulations weight the same active microclusters. The resulting partitions of the data space differ if and only if some component $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathbb{G}_0$ contains active microclusters that are connected exclusively through paths that traverse non-active microclusters. In that case, the single-graph formulation merges into one macrocluster the dense regions that the active graph keeps apart.
    \item \textbf{The Stated Purpose of the Criterion Requires the Second Pass}: The original work introduces the activation criterion to prevent the collapse of all microclusters into a single macrocluster under overlap (\cref{info:microcluster_activation_criterion}). Restricting the terms of \cref{eq:single_graph_typicality} does not split any component of $\mathbb{G}_0$, so a filter applied only to the calculations cannot prevent that collapse. The removal of the non-active microclusters from the graph is the only reading of the criterion under which its stated purpose is fulfilled.
    \item \textbf{The Single-Graph Membership Is Not Always Defined}: A component of $\mathbb{G}_0$ with $\mathcal{C} \cap A_k = \emptyset$ has a null normalizer in \cref{eq:single_graph_typicality}, and requires an additional convention to be excluded from \cref{eq:macrocluster_prediction}. Every component of $\mathbb{G}_1$ contains at least one active microcluster by construction, so its membership is always defined.
\end{enumerate}

Furthermore, the non-active microclusters that may act as bridges are precisely those subject to \cref{eq:activation_coupled_pruning}. An individual bridge persists only while its accumulated vitality lasts, unless it becomes active itself. The divergence between the two topologies is therefore expected to vanish in stationary streams, where the bridges are pruned and not replaced, and to persist in evolving streams, where drifting, merging, and overlapping concepts continuously create new low-density microclusters between dense regions.

To verify these properties empirically, two complementary evaluations were conducted. In the first, the planned correction was implemented as \modelname{single-graph}, which preserves the activation state required by the pruning routine but extracts the macroclusters from $\mathbb{G}_0$ and evaluates \cref{eq:single_graph_typicality}, assigning no label when no component has active microclusters. It was optimized and evaluated under the same protocol as the previous stages, with \modelname{corr-typicality} as its control. In the second, each run of \modelname{corr-typicality} was replayed with its optimized hyperparameters and, at every batch, the same model state was labelled under both topologies. Since both labellings derive from a single state, this replay isolates the effect of the topology from the effect of hyperparameter selection. \Cref{tab:macro_structure} reports the steady-state composition of the microcluster population, the steady-state number of macroclusters under each topology, the steady-state rate of unassigned samples, the agreement between the two labellings, measured as the Adjusted Rand Index between them and averaged over all batches, the Overall Adjusted Rand Index of each labelling against the ground truth, and the share of the fitting time spent in the activation pass. Due to computational constraints, both evaluations of this stage were restricted to the first five runs of each dataset. With five paired samples, the statistical procedure adopted in this work cannot reject any hypothesis at the adopted significance level, so no model is highlighted in the tables of this section and the results are discussed in terms of their consistency across datasets and runs.

\Cref{tab:macro_structure} confirms the analytical expectation and separates the datasets in two groups. In the stationary datasets, \ChameleonDSThree, \ChameleonDSFour, \datasetname{SCL-32}, and \datasetname{SCL-64}, both topologies extract the same number of macroclusters, the agreement between the labellings is complete or nearly so, and their clustering quality is identical. In the evolving streams, \datasetname{Stream Benchmark 1}, \datasetname{Stream Benchmark 2}, and \datasetname{Random RBF Generator with Events}, the single-graph topology consistently extracts fewer macroclusters from the same set of active microclusters, the agreement between the labellings drops to approximately $0.5$, and the quality obtained under $\mathbb{G}_0$ is lower than the one obtained under $\mathbb{G}_1$ in most runs. \Cassini lies between the two groups, with complete agreement in part of the runs and a loss of quality under $\mathbb{G}_0$ in the others. Because both labellings weight the same active microclusters of the same model state, these differences are exclusively attributable to the inactive bridges, which merge distinct dense regions into a single macrocluster. This is the collapse under overlap that the activation criterion was introduced to prevent, and only the second pass prevents it.

The table also shows that the rate of unassigned samples is null in every dataset, so the differences do not originate from components without active microclusters. Such components are nevertheless numerous under the single-graph formulation, since each immature microcluster that intersects no active one constitutes a component of its own, and immature microclusters outnumber the active ones in five of the eight datasets. Finally, the activation pass accounts for approximately $1\%$ to $2\%$ of the fitting time in all datasets. The significant cost attributed to the second pass by our reassessment is not confirmed.

The clustering quality of \modelname{single-graph} after its own hyperparameter optimization is presented in \cref{tab:macro_ari_overall,tab:macro_ari_fifth,tab:macro_ari_steadystate,tab:macro_ari_diff}. The optimization partially compensates for the loss observed in the replay, and no consistent difference to \modelname{corr-typicality} is observed: \modelname{single-graph} presents higher quality at the fifth batch in most datasets, similar or slightly higher Overall ARI, and lower Steady-State ARI in \datasetname{Stream Benchmark 2}, \Cassini, \ChameleonDSThree, and \ChameleonDSFour. The planned correction thus achieves no reliable improvement in clustering quality.

The compensation has a cost, shown in \cref{tab:macro_time_fit_cum_ms,tab:macro_time_predict_cum_ms,tab:macro_memory_max_bytes,tab:macro_memory_steadystate_bytes,tab:macro_mc_max,tab:macro_mc_steadystate}. The hyperparameters selected for \modelname{single-graph} sustain larger steady-state microcluster populations in every dataset, which increases its fitting time, prediction time, and steady-state memory footprint. The prediction time is further increased because the membership of a sample must be evaluated against every component of $\mathbb{G}_0$, including those without active microclusters. The suppression of the second pass therefore does not translate into any performance benefit.

We therefore revise our critique of the macrocluster construction. The second graph-processing pass is not a misinterpretation of the original formulation, but the topological expression of its activation criterion, and the only one under which the criterion fulfills its stated purpose. It guarantees that every macrocluster has a defined membership, it is consistent with the activation-coupled pruning retained in \cref{sec:inconsistent_decayment_logic}, and its removal yields neither quality nor performance benefits. Consequently, the planned correction is \textbf{not implemented}, and the two-pass topology of \cref{alg:microcluster_activation_criterion_pseudocode} is retained in the corrected model.
