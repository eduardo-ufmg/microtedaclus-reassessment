\section{Order-Dependent Characteristics}
\label{sec:order_dependent_characteristics}

An intuitive approach to solve the order-dependence of the microcluster density is to redefine it as an aggregation of the Typicality and Eccentricity Data Analytics variables across all aggregated samples. We show, however, that such solution is analytically flawed.

If

\begin{equation}
    V_k = \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2
\end{equation}

is the sum of squared Euclidean deviations for the $S_k$ samples of a microcluster $i$, for the standard spatial sample variance $\sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} V_k$,

\begin{equation}
    V_k = S_k \sigma^2_k
\end{equation}

Then, $\bar{\xi}_k$, the mean eccentricity of a microcluster $k$, is defined as \cref{eq:mean_eccentricity}.

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{1}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{S_k \sigma^2_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) = \frac{2}{S_k}
    \label{eq:mean_eccentricity}
\end{align}

If the exact recursive formulas from the original \MicroTEDAClus formulation are injected instead \cite{maia2020evolvclus}, the cancellation persists. The original formulation incorporates an anomalous penalty by the dimensionality $d$ squared in its variance computation, such that

\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{4\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{d^2}
\end{equation}

Which corresponts to $V_k = \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4}$. The original eccentricity formula is \cref{eq:original_eccentricity}.

\begin{equation}
    \xi(x_j) = \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d}
    \label{eq:original_eccentricity}
\end{equation}

Thus,

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2 V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \left( \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4} \right) \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{d}{2} \right)
\end{align}

In both cases, the spatial dispersion term $\sigma^2_k$ cancels out entirely. Because the geometric volume is analytically eliminated, the aggregated TEDA variables reduce strictly to a function of cardinality ($S_k$) and dimensionality ($d$). Consequently, any density metric derived from them collapses into a pure mass count, rendering the algorithm mathematically blind to actual spatial dispersion.

To formulate a geometrically valid and order-invariant density, the microcluster characterization is partitioned into intrinsic state variables and transient relational metrics. The TEDA variables $\xi(x)$, $\zeta(x)$, $\tau(x)$, and $t(x)$ are strictly preserved as point-to-set transient functions evaluated for an incoming sample $x$ against the distribution \cite{angelov2014outside}. They are explicitly removed from the microcluster's intrinsic state variables.

The microcluster state is thus restricted to its recursively updated sufficient statistics: $S_k$, $\mu_k$, and $\sigma^2_k$. Because the model characterizes the spatial dispersion of a $d$-dimensional microcluster using a single scalar variance $\sigma^2_k$ rather than a full covariance matrix, it mathematically enforces an isotropic geometric assumption. Under this assumption, the true spatial volume scales with $(\sigma^2_k)^{d/2}$. To avoid the numerical instability of exponential dimensionality scaling in continuous stream clustering, a linear proxy of variance is utilized as a standard algebraic relaxation \cite{cao2006density}. The microcluster density $D_k$ is redefined directly as the ratio of accumulated mass to this volumetric proxy in \cref{eq:corrected_density}.

\begin{equation}
    D_k = \frac{S_k}{\sigma^2_k + \epsilon}
    \label{eq:corrected_density}
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon > 0$ is a stabilization constant to prevent undefined behavior in single-sample microclusters. Furthermore, because a single-sample microcluster has zero empirical variance ($\sigma^2_k = 0$), calculating its density naively yields an artificial numerical singularity $D_k = 1/\epsilon$. In the baseline algorithm, macroclusters filter out low-density boundary elements by comparing each microcluster's density against the macrocluster mean density, $D^i_k \ge \text{mean}(D^m_k)$. If immature microclusters ($S_k \le 2$) are naively included in this arithmetic mean, the $1/\epsilon$ singularity inflates $\text{mean}(D^m_k)$ to millions, artificially deactivating all mature units in the concept. To preserve analytical consistency, the macrocluster activation threshold is evaluated exclusively over mature microclusters:

\begin{equation}
    \bar{D}_k^m = \frac{1}{|M_k^{m, \text{mature}}|} \sum_{i \in M_k^{m, \text{mature}}} D_k^i,\quad M_k^{m, \text{mature}} = \{i \in M_k^m \mid S_k^i > 2\}
    \label{eq:mature_mean_density}
\end{equation}

insulating the activation threshold from newborn initialization artifacts.

\Cref{tab:orderdep_ari_overall,tab:orderdep_ari_fifth,tab:orderdep_ari_steadystate,tab:orderdep_ari_diff} present the clustering quality impact of the correction. While the redefinition results in lower initial and overall ARI scores on several streams, it preserves or improves steady-state clustering quality once the microclusters sufficiently mature. In the baseline implementation, the recursive, order-dependent density formula accumulated historical artifacts that acted as an artificial memory mechanism, providing path-dependent inertia during early and transitioning stream phases. By replacing this with a memoryless volumetric snapshot ratio, the model becomes strictly responsive to current spatial dispersion.

\section{Dimensionality Penalty}
\label{sec:dimensionality_penalty}

In the original formulation \cite{maia2020evolvclus}, the spatial dispersion metrics explicitly penalize the Euclidean norm by the data dimensionality $d$. Specifically, the recursive variance $\left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2$ and the point-to-set eccentricity $\xi^i(x_k)$ arbitrarily scale the squared deviations by $4/d^2$ and $2/d$, respectively. This scaling factor is analytically flawed. It distorts the true geometric boundaries of the microclusters and deviates from the standard TEDA derivations for the Euclidean space \cite{angelov2014outside}, where the spatial variance strictly reflects the unscaled trace of the covariance matrix.

To restore the analytical integrity of the spatial boundaries, the dimensionality penalty is removed. The recursive variance update for a microcluster $i$ at moment $k$ is corrected to the standard formulation:

$$ \left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2 = \frac{S^i_k - 1}{S^i_k} \left(\sigma^i_{k-1}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{S^i_k - 1} \lVert x_k - \mu^i_k \rVert^2 $$

Correspondingly, the transient point-to-set eccentricity evaluation is restored to its exact unscaled geometric form:

$$ \xi^i(x_k) = \frac{1}{S^i_k} + \frac{\lVert \mu^i_k - x_k \rVert^2}{S^i_k \left(\sigma^i_k\right)^2} $$

As shown in \cref{tab:dimpen_ari_overall,tab:dimpen_ari_fifth}, the baseline continues to achieve higher overall and initial ARI on low-dimensional streams. This behavior is mathematically expected for two-dimensional datasets, where the dimensionality penalty factor $2/d = 1$ is an identity operation, yielding virtually identical clustering dynamics between the order-independent and penalty-free formulations. Crucially, in higher-dimensional feature spaces, \cref{tab:dimpen_ari_steadystate,tab:dimpen_ari_diff} demonstrate a profound qualitative improvement: while the baseline suffers catastrophic late-stream instability and severe steady-state degradation, removing the dimensionality penalty preserves geometric boundary fidelity and yields a stable, non-degrading steady-state partition.

\section{Correction of the Typicality Formulation}
\label{sec:correction_typicality}

The evaluation of the original implementation reveals a critical mathematical discrepancy in the computation of a sample's membership to a macrocluster. The algorithm assigns a sample $x_k$ to a macrocluster by weighting the typicality of the sample against the active microclusters. However, the implemented typicality formulation evaluates an unnormalized intermediate expression, equivalent to $1 - \zeta^i(x_k)$. This expression contradicts the foundational definitions of the TEDA framework \cite{angelov2014outside}. In the formal mathematical model, absolute typicality is strictly defined as $\tau^i(x_k) = 1 - \xi^i(x_k)$. Furthermore, to construct valid density-based membership probabilities, the aggregation must utilize normalized typicality, defined as:

\begin{equation}
    t^i(x_k) = \frac{\tau^i(x_k)}{S^i_k - 2}
\end{equation}

By omitting the cardinality normalization factor $S^i_k - 2$, the original algorithm fails to appropriately scale the typicality across microclusters of varying sizes, inherently invalidating the resulting membership distributions. To restore theoretical consistency, the membership calculation is corrected to explicitly compute the exact normalized typicality $t^i(x_k)$. It is important to note that this normalized formulation is mathematically defined exclusively for microclusters containing more than two samples ($S^i_k > 2$). This domain constraint is naturally satisfied in the execution pipeline; because the macrocluster activation condition strictly filters out microclusters with $S^i_k \le 2$ prior to the membership evaluation, the denominator is mathematically guaranteed to be strictly positive.

As reported in \cref{tab:math_ari_overall,tab:math_ari_fifth,tab:math_ari_steadystate,tab:math_ari_diff,tab:math_time_fit_cum_ms,tab:math_time_predict_cum_ms,tab:math_memory_max_bytes,tab:math_memory_steadystate_bytes,tab:math_mc_max,tab:math_mc_steadystate}, restoring the mathematically exact normalized typicality produced no statistically significant shifts across clustering quality, runtime, or memory consumption. Analytically, while the normalization factor $1/(S^i_k - 2)$ is strictly necessary to construct valid probability densities under the TEDA framework, it primarily rescales the relative weighting among active microclusters within the same macrocluster. Because macrocluster assignments are fundamentally dictated by the spatial connectivity of the underlying graph and the nearest mature microclusters, rescaling intra-cluster typicalities preserves the relative ranking between competing macroclusters.

\section{Inconsistent Decayment Logic}
\label{sec:inconsistent_decayment_logic}

The original \MicroTEDAClus formulation introduces no explicit mechanism for data decay or memory pruning \cite{maia2020evolvclus}. However, analysis of the underlying model execution reveals the presence of an undocumented decay heuristic based on the Euclidean distance of a sample relative to a microcluster's standard deviation. Furthermore, pruning is strictly coupled to a microcluster's transient activation status during the macrocluster graph formation.

These heuristics diverge significantly from the formal decay logic introduced in the updated thesis formulation \cite{galindo2021lastmile}, which specifies a strict linear penalty $v^i_k = v^i_{k-1} - \rho$ applied exclusively when an incoming sample $x_k$ does not update the microcluster $i$. While mathematically formalized, this static penalty remains analytically flawed for continuous data streams. Because it applies a constant subtraction $\rho$ independent of the accumulated mass, mature microclusters representing major concepts are deleted at the exact same rate as transient noise buffers.

To establish a memory management mechanism that does not invalidates the strict unweighted arithmetic expansion required by the Typicality and Eccentricity Data Analytics sufficient statistics \cite{angelov2014outside}, the decay parameter must be isolated and made proportional to the microcluster's inertia. The distance-based heuristics and the dependence on macrocluster activation are entirely removed. The decay parameter $v^i_k$ is refreshed to $1.0$ upon a successful sample match. If the sample is an outlier to the microcluster, an inertia-weighted penalty is applied:

\begin{equation}
    v^i_k = v^i_{k-1} - \frac{\rho}{S^i_k}
\end{equation}

where $\rho > 0$ is a static fading factor. A microcluster is independently pruned from memory when $v^i_k \le 0$. Under this formulation, transient noise microclusters decay rapidly, while mature microclusters retain significant inertia, enabling them to survive temporary periods of inactivity and correctly identify recurring concepts.

As reported in \cref{tab:decay_ari_overall,tab:decay_ari_fifth,tab:decay_ari_steadystate,tab:decay_ari_diff}, the inertia-weighted decay correction severely degraded clustering quality across virtually all evaluation metrics. While theoretically designed to safeguard mature microclusters from premature deletion, applying a penalty proportional to $1/S^i_k$ introduces a pathological retention bias in non-stationary continuous streams: mature microclusters with large sample counts receive negligible penalties per outlier sample. In practice, this grants obsolete spatial concepts near-infinite lifespans, preventing the model from adapting when underlying cluster boundaries drift.

\section{Macrocluster Topology and Graph Processing}
\label{sec:macrocluster_topology}

The original \MicroTEDAClus algorithm defines macroclusters as disjoint sets of connected microclusters, where connectivity is determined by the spatial intersection condition

\begin{equation}
    \text{dist}(\mu^i_k, \mu^j_k) < 2 \left( \sigma^i_k + \sigma^j_k \right)
\end{equation}

To prevent microcluster collapse during evaluations, the original formulation specifies that a microcluster participates in macrocluster calculations only if its density exceeds the macrocluster's mean density, $D^l_k \ge \text{mean}(D^l_k)$.

The baseline implementation misinterprets this calculation filter as a strict topological constraint. It executes a computationally expensive two-pass graph reconstruction: first building the base intersection graph $\mathbb{G}_0$, and subsequently severing nodes that fall below the mean density to extract an active graph $\mathbb{G}_1$. This approach artificially fractures cohesive macroscopic data concepts into disconnected sub-clusters. Furthermore, continuously duplicating network graphs and repeatedly solving for connected components across the microcluster space introduces an unnecessary computational bottleneck during continuous stream processing.

To restore topological integrity and resolve the computational overhead, we decouple the macrocluster topology from the transient activation logic. The structural graph is maintained strictly as the single intersection graph $\mathbb{G}_0$, preserving the continuous geometric boundaries of the underlying data concepts. The activation threshold is subsequently relocated to the prediction phase.

When calculating a sample's membership to a macrocluster $\mathcal{M}_j$, the algorithm dynamically computes the mean density of $\mathcal{M}_j$. Only the microclusters satisfying both the maturity constraint $S^i_k > 2$ and the density threshold

\begin{equation}
    D^i_k \ge \text{mean}(D^m_k),\quad m \in \mathcal{M}_j
\end{equation}

are dynamically passed to the typicality weighting function. If a macrocluster possesses no active microclusters, its membership is formally undefined and set to $-\infty$, ensuring it cannot erroneously win assignment over active clusters when external points exhibit negative normalized typicalities. If all macroclusters are inactive for an incoming point, the sample is labeled as unassigned, $-1$. This structural alignment fulfills the original mathematical intent of filtering sparse boundary elements without destructively fragmenting the underlying macrocluster graph.

The clustering quality evaluations in \cref{tab:macro_ari_overall,tab:macro_ari_fifth,tab:macro_ari_steadystate,tab:macro_ari_diff} reveal a pronounced dichotomy across stream dimensionalities. In higher-dimensional streams, preserving graph continuity in the base intersection graph $\mathbb{G}_0$ prevents cohesive macroscopic structures from being artificially severed into fragmented sub-clusters; on these streams, the model achieves the highest overall ARI scores observed across the entire iterative progression, accompanied by near-zero ARI degradation. Conversely, on lower-dimensional streams, relocating the activation threshold $D^i_k \ge \text{mean}(D^m_k)$ to prediction time exposes an unforeseen structural vulnerability: within smaller, sparse, or transitioning macroclusters, enforcing a local arithmetic mean density threshold frequently leaves zero active microclusters. Samples arriving in these regions cannot be assigned valid membership, resulting in unassigned classifications and causing the overall ARI to collapse on low-dimensional streams.

\section{Summary and Discussion}
\label{sec:correction_summary}

The iterative corrections evaluated throughout this chapter resolved the critical mathematical and architectural discrepancies identified in the legacy \MicroTEDAClus implementation. By removing the recursive historical bias from microcluster density, restoring unscaled Euclidean dispersion metrics, normalizing typicality according to the TEDA framework, and decoupling the macrocluster graph from transient activations, the model was successfully brought into strict alignment with its formal theoretical foundations. These corrections achieved marked empirical success in high-dimensional feature spaces: while the baseline suffers catastrophic late-stream collapse in higher dimensions, the corrected formulation eliminated steady-state degradation and achieved the highest overall ARI scores across the entire evaluation. Furthermore, eliminating the two-pass graph reconstruction resolved the severe computational bottlenecks of the legacy implementation, restoring bounded memory footprints and fast online fitting times.

However, the empirical evaluations also reveal that faithfully correcting individual implementation flaws worsened clustering quality on lower-dimensional streams. This outcome indicates that the original implementation relies on a web of mutually compensating tweaks: the artificial inertia of the order-dependent density and the destructive pruning of the two-pass active graph masked fundamental conceptual weaknesses in the algorithm. Specifically, the literature-mandated inertia decay $v - \rho/S$ grants mature microclusters near-immortality, preventing the timely retirement of obsolete concepts when distributions drift. Simultaneously, relocating the arithmetic mean density threshold $D \ge \text{mean}(D)$ to the prediction phase frequently starves sparse or evolving macroclusters of active microclusters, leaving arriving points unassigned and collapsing low-dimensional clustering scores.

Rather than indicating a failure of theoretical rigor, these results demonstrate that fixing implementation bugs alone cannot overcome flawed heuristic design choices. By stripping away undocumented heuristics and establishing an order-invariant, mathematically transparent model, the corrections provide an essential baseline where component behaviors are cleanly isolated. The specific failure modes exposed in this chapter provide the direct empirical and analytical foundation for the dedicated algorithmic improvements developed in \cref{chap:ImprovedModel}.
