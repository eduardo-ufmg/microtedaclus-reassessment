\section{Order-Dependent Characteristics}
\label{sec:order_dependent_characteristics}

An intuitive approach to solve the order-dependence of the microcluster density is to redefine it as an aggregation of the Typicality and Eccentricity Data Analytics variables across all aggregated samples. We show, however, that such solution is analytically flawed.

If

\begin{equation}
    V_k = \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2
\end{equation}

is the sum of squared Euclidean deviations for the $S_k$ samples of a microcluster $i$, for the standard spatial sample variance $\sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} V_k$,

\begin{equation}
    V_k = S_k \sigma^2_k
\end{equation}

Then, $\bar{\xi}_k$, the mean eccentricity of a microcluster $k$, is defined as \cref{eq:mean_eccentricity}.

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{1}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{S_k \sigma^2_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k} \right) = \frac{2}{S_k}
    \label{eq:mean_eccentricity}
\end{align}

If the exact recursive formulas from the original \MicroTEDAClus formulation are injected instead \cite{maia2020evolvclus}, the cancellation persists. The original formulation incorporates an anomalous penalty by the dimensionality $d$ squared in its variance computation, such that

\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_k = \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \frac{4\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{d^2}
\end{equation}

Which corresponts to $V_k = \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4}$. The original eccentricity formula is \cref{eq:original_eccentricity}.

\begin{equation}
    \xi(x_j) = \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d}
    \label{eq:original_eccentricity}
\end{equation}

Thus,

\begin{align}
    \bar{\xi}_k &= \frac{1}{S_k} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \left( \frac{1}{S_k} + \frac{2\lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \sum_{j=1}^{S_k} \lVert \mu_k - x_j \rVert^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2 V_k}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{S_k \sigma^2_k d} \left( \frac{d^2 S_k \sigma^2_k}{4} \right) \right) \nonumber \\
                &= \frac{1}{S_k} \left( 1 + \frac{d}{2} \right)
\end{align}

In both cases, the spatial dispersion term $\sigma^2_k$ cancels out entirely. Because the geometric volume is analytically eliminated, the aggregated TEDA variables reduce strictly to a function of cardinality $S_k$ and dimensionality $d$. Consequently, any density metric derived from them collapses into a pure mass count, rendering the algorithm mathematically blind to actual spatial dispersion.

To formulate a geometrically valid and order-invariant density, the microcluster characterization is partitioned into intrinsic state variables and transient relational metrics. The TEDA variables $\xi(x)$, $\zeta(x)$, $\tau(x)$, and $t(x)$ are strictly preserved as point-to-set transient functions evaluated for an incoming sample $x$ against the distribution \cite{angelov2014outside}. They are explicitly removed from the microcluster's intrinsic state variables.

The microcluster state is thus restricted to its recursively updated sufficient statistics: $S_k$, $\mu_k$, and $\sigma^2_k$. Because the model characterizes the spatial dispersion of a $d$-dimensional microcluster using a single scalar variance $\sigma^2_k$ rather than a full covariance matrix, it mathematically enforces an isotropic geometric assumption. Under this assumption, the true spatial volume scales with $(\sigma^2_k)^{d/2}$. To avoid the numerical instability of exponential dimensionality scaling in continuous stream clustering, a linear proxy of variance is utilized as a standard algebraic relaxation.

However, evaluating density directly as a ratio of cardinality to variance exposes a fundamental statistical constraint during the cluster initialization phase. In classical statistical estimation, sample variance possesses $S_k - 1$ degrees of freedom; for a single discrete observation, spatial dispersion is mathematically undefined. For a pair of points, the variance reflects only an unrepresentative collinear displacement, and the TEDA dynamic outlier boundary $m(k)$ does not commence until $S_k \ge 3$. Evaluating an uninitialized singleton unit with a standard denominator stabilization parameter $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ naively yields an artificial numerical singularity $D_k = 1/\epsilon = 10^8$. In a streaming environment, assigning a newborn candidate unit a density millions of times larger than well-established clusters is physically ungrounded.

In accordance with the baseline implementation, the order-independent microcluster density $D_k$ is formally defined as a piecewise function in \cref{eq:corrected_density}.

\begin{equation}
    D_k = \begin{cases}
        0, & S_k < 3 \\
        \dfrac{S_k}{\sigma^2_k + \epsilon}, & S_k \ge 3
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:corrected_density}
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon > 0$ serves strictly as a numerical regularization constant guarding against division-by-zero for collinear or identical samples in mature clusters. By defining $D_k = 0$ for $S_k < 3$ directly at the microcluster level, the state invariants remain strictly bounded, eliminating the unphysical $1/\epsilon$ singularity at its source.

Within macroclusters, the activation threshold filters out low-density boundary elements by comparing each microcluster's density against the concept's mean density. To ensure that incubating units undergoing statistical accumulation do not dilute or distort this threshold, the macrocluster mean density is evaluated exclusively across mature microclusters:

\begin{equation}
    \bar{D}_k^m = \begin{cases}
        \dfrac{1}{|M_k^{m, \text{mature}}|} \displaystyle\sum_{i \in M_k^{m, \text{mature}}} D_k^i, & \text{if } |M_k^{m, \text{mature}}| > 0 \\
        0, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:mature_mean_density}
\end{equation}

where $M_k^{m, \text{mature}} = \{i \in M_k^m \mid S_k^i \ge 3\}$. A microcluster $i \in M_k^m$ is subsequently designated as active if and only if it is mature and exceeds the macrocluster average:

\begin{equation}
    \text{active}_k^i = (S_k^i \ge 3) \land (D_k^i \ge \bar{D}_k^m)
    \label{eq:microcluster_activation}
\end{equation}

Microclusters failing this criterion are excluded from the active graph $\mathbb{G}_1$. This formulation resolves the order-dependent bias of the original model while insulating the active concept boundaries from newborn initialization artifacts.

This correction results in a slight decrease in clustering quality, as shown by \cref{tab:ari_overall,tab:ari_fifth,tab:ari_steadystate,tab:ari_diff}.
