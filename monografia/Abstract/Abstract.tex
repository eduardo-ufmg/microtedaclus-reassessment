\begin{center}
\huge{{\bf Abstract}}
\vspace{1cm}
\end{center}

MicroTEDAClus is an evolving clustering algorithm for data streams that summarizes the stream into microclusters by the eccentricity and the typicality of the TEDA framework, and whose authors report that it performs on par with established stream clustering algorithms and adapts better than them to changes in the data. In this undergraduate thesis, developed at the Federal University of Minas Gerais in the Bachelor's Degree in Control and Automation Engineering, under the supervision of Prof. Janier Arias Garcia, we reassess the algorithm and propose an improved model. We find issues in the formulation of the algorithm, differences between the formulation and the public implementation that produced its published results, and flaws in the methodology of its experiments, among them preprocessing steps that leak information about the data and an evaluation that predicts each batch of samples after learning it. Under a methodology without these flaws, in which we execute every model in the same language on the unprocessed data, evaluate it prequentially, tune it by the same automatic search and compare the models over many runs by nonparametric statistical tests, MicroTEDAClus is not on par with the benchmark models of the original work, nor does it adapt better than them to concept drift.

We obtain the improved model, MicroTEDAClus+, from controlled experiments in which we accept each correction or modification by a predefined rule, on runs separate from those of its evaluation. On the synthetic datasets of the original work it improves on MicroTEDAClus in clustering quality, execution time and memory, no benchmark model is better than it in the datasets with concept drift, and it is the fastest model of that experiment. In an experiment modernized in its environment, its benchmark models and its selection of hyperparameters, it is better than every benchmark model in some datasets and worse than most of them in others. On eight real datasets, under the same protocol, it is better than every benchmark model in one dataset and worse than most of them in four, predicts at least as fast as all of them, and its summary outgrows its limit in the longest stream.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Data stream clustering. Evolving clustering. TEDA. Concept drift. Experimental methodology.
