\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

In the previous chapters we report each experiment on its own terms; in this chapter we read them together: what they establish about the claims of the original work, how the model behaves on synthetic and on real data, how much of each comparison depends on the protocol that selects the hyperparameters, and where the evidence stops.

\section{The Claims of the Original Work}
\label{sec:disc_claims}

The original work reports that \MicroTEDAClus performs on par with established stream clustering algorithms and adapts to concept drift better than them. Under the reassessed methodology, neither claim holds. Against the established benchmark models of the original work, \DenStream, \CluStream and \StreamKM, \MicroTEDAClus is worse than \CluStream and \StreamKM in the Overall ARI of the three static datasets, and at the penultimate batch it is better than none of the three in any dataset and worse than \StreamKM in all six (\cref{sec:reassessment_results}). In two of the three streams with concept drift, its median Steady-State ARI is zero. It learns more slowly than every benchmark model but \AutoCloud, and in the scalability streams of high dimension its microclusters collapse along the stream, which we trace to the dimensionality penalty of the formulation (\cref{subsubsec:dimensionality_penalty}).

The published results were obtained under the conditions that we found in the original methodology (\cref{chap:methodology_reassessment}). The datasets were cleaned of noise, scaled with statistics of the whole stream and ordered by label, which made the clustering of most batches trivial (\cref{sec:datasets}); the evaluation learned each batch before predicting it (\cref{sec:prequential_sequential_offline_evaluation}); the benchmark models ran through layers of interface that inflated their times (\cref{fig:benchmark_java_bridge}); and the implementation that produced the results departs from the formulation of the paper (\cref{sec:model_implementation}). The published comparisons therefore measured these conditions together with the models. The reassessed methodology removes them for every model alike, and under it the original model does not confirm the results published for it.

\section{Synthetic and Real Data}
\label{sec:disc_data}

On the synthetic datasets, \MicroTEDAClusPlus corrected the weaknesses of the original model. It is better than \MicroTEDAClus in the Overall ARI of five of the six datasets and in the Steady-State ARI of all six, faster in both stages and smaller in memory; no benchmark model is better than it in the streams with concept drift, and it is the fastest model of the main experiment (\cref{subsec:mi_main_summary}). Its weakness is the static shapes, where \CluStream and \StreamKM remain better. The modernized experiment, with other benchmark models, longer streams and the selection by rules, moves its position. It remains better than every benchmark model in the Overall ARI of \BenchmarkOne and not worse than any in the scalability streams from two dimensions on, but every benchmark model is better than it in \BenchmarkTwo, and it predicts faster than all of them in most datasets without learning faster (\cref{sec:mod_summary}). On the real datasets, the same model is worse than most benchmark models in six of the eight (\cref{tab:real_quality}). These datasets differ from the synthetic ones in what \cref{chap:real_data} names: their drifts come from the conditions of the recording, their classes are not drawn from chosen distributions, and their dimension reaches the hundreds.

The diagnosis of \cref{sec:ri_diagnosis} locates the failure in two mechanisms rather than in the structure of the model. In the INSECTS datasets and in \Pendigits most microclusters never mature, so the activation, the macroclusters and the prediction rest on a few of them, and the noise label marks as noise samples of classes that cover the whole stream (\cref{tab:ri_diagnosis}). The search also placed the variance limit at the lower end of its range in most runs of three datasets (\cref{tab:ri_variance}): the model called for smaller microclusters than its range allowed. The changes that answer these mechanisms, a lower range of the variance limit and the removal of the noise label, carry most of the gains of the final version (\cref{tab:ri_chain}). Both concern choices that depend on the data, the range of a hyperparameter and the assumption of a noise class, and not the algorithm.

The structure of the model withstood its alternatives a second time. The function $m$, the isotropic variance, the density of the last absorbed sample, the activation criterion and the membership by the nearest microcluster, which we retained from the synthetic experiments (\cref{sec:mi_final_model}), faced alternatives again on the real data, and we kept none of them (\cref{tab:ri_candidates}).

The final version narrows the gap without closing it. It is worse than most benchmark models in three real datasets instead of six, better than every one in \Shuttle and not worse than any in \Pendigits (\cref{tab:ri_quality,tab:real_quality}). But it remains worse than \DenStream and \BICO in the three INSECTS datasets, the real streams with concept drift, it starts slowly (\cref{fig:ri_slow_start}), and it splits the classes of the INSECTS datasets over many clusters (\cref{tab:ri_partitions}). The model that no benchmark model surpassed in the synthetic streams with concept drift falls behind in the real ones.

The two versions of \MicroTEDAClusPlus fit different data. The version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} labels noise, which improved the model on the synthetic datasets with noise (\cref{sec:mi_improvements}) and costs it on real datasets whose classes cover every sample. The final version drops the label, and we did not evaluate it on the synthetic datasets (\cref{subsec:ri_limits}). Neither version has been shown to serve both kinds of data.

\section{The Weight of the Protocol}
\label{sec:disc_protocol}

Every result that we report measures a model as its protocol tunes it, and the protocol weighs on the results as much as some changes to the model do. The probe of \cref{sec:mi_selection} found configurations in the search space of \MicroTEDAClusPlus far better than those that the search selected. The modernized protocol raised the results of the same model in about half of the datasets without any change to it, most of all at the penultimate batch of \BenchmarkOne and \Cassini (\cref{tab:mod_protocols}), and in the Chameleon datasets it selected the fine-grained configurations that the probe had found (\cref{subsec:mod_cost}). On the real data, the first kept change widens a search range (\cref{tab:ri_variance}), and the losses of the link factor come from a search with one more hyperparameter and the same number of trials (\cref{subsec:ri_link}). A comparison between models is thus a comparison between models and their selection, which is why we tune every model of a comparison by the same protocol.

The search is also a source of variation. Two searches of a single algorithm give medians that differ without significance (\cref{tab:mi_refinements_tuning_noise}), and the final version has different medians in the last step of the confirmation and in its own experiment, whose searches differ in their seeds (\cref{tab:ri_confirmation,tab:ri_quality}). Differences of that size are not effects of a model, and the tests over many runs are what separate the effects from this variation.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}
\label{sec:disc_limits}

Five limits qualify these results.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Streams that repeat.} The Chameleon datasets are fixed files in the main experiment (\cref{subsec:mi_runs}), and the INSECTS datasets and \Covertype keep the order of their files (\cref{subsec:ri_runs}). Their runs repeat the search and not the stream, so on them the validation of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} and the confirmation of \cref{chap:real_improvement} confirm the outcome of the search more than that of the model.
    \item \textbf{Number of runs.} The real-data experiment of \cref{chap:real_data} rests on 32 runs instead of 94 (\cref{sec:real_protocol}), the cost of the final version and of the benchmark models on 32, and the cost of the modernized experiment on ten (\cref{sec:mod_measurements}). With fewer runs the tests separate fewer models.
    \item \textbf{Models and runs left out.} \BIRCH fails in most runs of three real datasets, and we leave it out of them (\cref{tab:real_failures}), and the limit on the summary ends some runs of the final version, most of them in \Covertype (\cref{tab:ri_failures}). The comparisons of these datasets rest on the runs that every remaining model finished.
    \item \textbf{Cost.} The execution time and the memory measure implementations and not only algorithms, as \cref{subsec:mi_main_cost} notes and as the reimplementation of \cref{sec:c_implementation} shows for a single algorithm, and the models of a comparison are implemented in different libraries.
    \item \textbf{Datasets.} The synthetic datasets are streams and shapes of two dimensions and scalability streams of up to sixty-four, and the real datasets are eight, chosen by the criteria of \cref{sec:real_datasets}, one of them criticized for its redundant records. Our conclusions about real data extend to data of that kind and not beyond.
\end{itemize}
