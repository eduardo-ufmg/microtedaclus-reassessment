\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

The previous chapters report each experiment on its own terms. This chapter reads them together: what they establish about the claims of the original work, how the model behaves on synthetic and on real data, how much of each comparison depends on the protocol that selects the hyperparameters, and where the evidence stops.

\section{The Claims of the Original Work}
\label{sec:disc_claims}

The original work reports that \MicroTEDAClus performs on par with established stream clustering algorithms and adapts to concept drift better than them. Under the reassessed methodology, neither claim is supported in full: \MicroTEDAClus is outperformed by the benchmark models in some metrics and roughly equivalent to them in others. Among the models of the original work, \MicroTEDAClus is the sole worst model in the Overall ARI of \BenchmarkOne, a dataset with concept drift, it is indistinguishable from the worst model at the penultimate batch and in the maximum ARI drop of every dataset, and it is the sole slowest model in the fitting stage, \cref{sec:reassessment_results}. In the scalability streams of high dimension its microclusters collapse along the stream, which \cref{sec:reassessment_results} traces to the dimensionality penalty of the formulation, \cref{subsubsec:dimensionality_penalty}.

The published results were obtained under the conditions that \cref{chap:methodology_reassessment} found in the original methodology. The datasets were cleaned of noise, scaled with statistics of the whole stream and ordered by label, which made the clustering of most batches trivial, \cref{sec:datasets}; the evaluation learned each batch before predicting it, \cref{sec:prequential_sequential_offline_evaluation}; the benchmark models ran through layers of interface that inflated their times, \cref{fig:benchmark_java_bridge}; and the implementation that produced the results departs from the formulation of the paper, \cref{sec:model_implementation}. The published comparisons therefore measured these conditions together with the models. The reassessed methodology removes them for every model alike, and under it the original model does not confirm the results published for it.

\section{Synthetic and Real Data}
\label{sec:disc_data}

On the synthetic datasets, \MicroTEDAClusPlus corrected the weaknesses of the original model. It improves on \MicroTEDAClus in clustering quality, execution time and memory, \cref{sec:mi_main_experiment}, it is among the best benchmark models in the datasets with concept drift and the fastest of them, \cref{sec:results_summary}, and in the modernized experiment it remains indistinguishable from the best model in most datasets, \cref{sec:mod_summary}. On the real datasets, the same model is indistinguishable from the worst model in six of the eight, \cref{tab:real_quality}. These datasets differ from the synthetic ones in what \cref{chap:real_data} names: their drifts come from the conditions of the recording, their classes are not drawn from chosen distributions, and their dimension reaches the hundreds.

The diagnosis of \cref{sec:ri_diagnosis} locates the failure in two mechanisms rather than in the structure of the model. In the INSECTS datasets and in \Pendigits most microclusters never mature, so the activation, the macroclusters and the prediction rest on a few of them, and the noise label marks as noise samples of classes that cover the whole stream, \cref{tab:ri_diagnosis}. The search also placed the variance limit at the lower end of its range in most runs of three datasets, \cref{tab:ri_variance}: the model called for smaller microclusters than its range allowed. The changes that answer these mechanisms, a lower range of the variance limit and the removal of the noise label, carry most of the gains of the final version, \cref{tab:ri_chain}. Both concern choices that depend on the data, the range of a hyperparameter and the assumption of a noise class, and not the algorithm.

The structure of the model withstood its alternatives a second time. The function $m$, the isotropic variance, the density of the last absorbed sample, the activation criterion and the membership by the nearest microcluster, which \cref{sec:mi_final_model} retained from the synthetic experiments, were challenged again on the real data, and no alternative to them was kept, \cref{tab:ri_candidates}.

The final version narrows the gap without closing it. It is indistinguishable from the worst model in three real datasets instead of six and from the best in \Shuttle, \cref{tab:ri_quality}, but it remains behind the best models on the INSECTS datasets and \GasDrift, the real streams with concept drift, it still starts slowly, and it splits the classes of the INSECTS datasets over many clusters, \cref{tab:ri_partitions}. The model that leads among the benchmark models in the synthetic streams with concept drift is not among the best in the real ones.

The two versions of \MicroTEDAClusPlus fit different data. The version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} labels noise, which improved the model on the synthetic datasets with noise, \cref{sec:mi_improvements}, and costs it on real datasets whose classes cover every sample. The final version drops the label, and it was not evaluated on the synthetic datasets, \cref{subsec:ri_limits}. Neither version has been shown to serve both kinds of data.

\section{The Weight of the Protocol}
\label{sec:disc_protocol}

Every result of this work measures a model as its protocol tunes it, and the protocol weighs on the results as much as some changes to the model do. The probe of \cref{sec:mi_selection} found configurations in the search space of \MicroTEDAClusPlus far better than those that the search selected. The modernized protocol raised the results of the same model in most datasets without any change to it, \cref{tab:mod_protocols}. On the real data, the first kept change widens a search range, \cref{tab:ri_variance}, and the losses of the link factor come from a search with one more hyperparameter and the same number of trials, \cref{subsec:ri_link}. A comparison between models is thus a comparison between models and their selection, which is why every model of a comparison is tuned by the same protocol.

The search is also a source of variation. Two searches of one same algorithm give medians that differ without significance, \cref{tab:mi_refinements_tuning_noise}, and the final version has different medians in the last step of the confirmation and in its own experiment, whose searches differ in their seeds, \cref{tab:ri_confirmation,tab:ri_quality}. Differences of that size are not effects of a model, and the tests over many runs are what separate the effects from this variation.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}
\label{sec:disc_limits}

Five limits qualify these results.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Streams that repeat.} The Chameleon datasets are fixed files, \cref{subsec:mi_runs}, and the INSECTS datasets and \Covertype keep the order of their files, \cref{subsec:ri_runs}. Their runs repeat the search and not the stream, so on them the validation of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} and the confirmation of \cref{chap:real_improvement} confirm the outcome of the search more than that of the model.
    \item \textbf{Number of runs.} The real-data experiment rests on 32 runs instead of 94, \cref{sec:real_protocol}, and the cost of the final version and of the benchmark models on ten. With fewer runs the tests separate fewer models.
    \item \textbf{Models and runs left out.} \BIRCH fails in most runs of three real datasets and is left out of them, \cref{tab:real_failures}, and the limit on the summary ends some runs of the final version, most of them in \Covertype, \cref{tab:ri_failures}. The comparisons of these datasets rest on the runs that every remaining model finished.
    \item \textbf{Cost.} The execution time and the memory measure implementations and not only algorithms, as \cref{sec:results_time} notes, and the models of a comparison are implemented in different libraries.
    \item \textbf{Datasets.} The synthetic datasets are streams and shapes of two dimensions and scalability streams of up to sixty-four, and the real datasets are eight, chosen by the criteria of \cref{sec:real_datasets}, one of them criticised for its redundant records. The conclusions about real data extend to data of that kind and not beyond.
\end{itemize}
