\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

Data streams arrive continuously, without a known end, from processes that can change while they are observed. Clustering them requires an algorithm that processes each sample as it arrives, keeps a bounded summary and follows the changes of the data, a family of methods known as stream or evolving clustering \cite{aggarwal2003framework,cao2006density,wang2023data}. \MicroTEDAClus \cite{maia2020evolvclus} is one such algorithm. It summarizes the stream into microclusters by the eccentricity and the typicality of the TEDA framework \cite{angelov2014outside}, which assume no distribution for the data, and groups them into macroclusters, the clusters of the stream. Its authors report that it performs on par with established stream clustering algorithms, and that it adapts better than them to concept drift.

In this work, we reassess \MicroTEDAClus and propose an improved model. We find issues in the formulation of the algorithm, differences between it and the implementation that produced the published results, and flaws in the methodology of the original experiments, among them a preprocessing that leaks information about the stream and an evaluation that predicts each batch after learning it. Under a methodology that corrects these flaws, \MicroTEDAClus is not on par with the benchmark models of the original work, nor does it adapt better than them to concept drift. We obtain the improved model, \MicroTEDAClusPlus, from controlled experiments on corrections and modifications of the algorithm. On the datasets of the original work it improves on \MicroTEDAClus in clustering quality, execution time and memory, no benchmark model is better than it in the datasets with concept drift, and it is the fastest model of the experiment, while two benchmark models remain better than it in the static shapes.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The results of \MicroTEDAClus are the basis of later work. The doctoral thesis of Galindo~\cite{galindo2021lastmile} updates the algorithm and applies it to the last-mile problem in logistics, without examining the original evaluation. The undergraduate thesis of Cardoso~\cite{cardoso2022microtedaclus} reimplements it in \codename{C} to reduce its execution time, evaluates its quality only on static datasets that the model has already learned, and expects it to handle concept drift because the original work reports that it does. A model whose evaluation supports further work must be verified: information leaked from the data into the evaluation, among other errors, has been found to inflate the published results of many studies in machine learning \cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}. The algorithm is also attractive: the TEDA metrics require neither a distribution for the data nor a predefined number of clusters.

The TEDA framework is that of Angelov~\cite{angelov2014outside}. The formulation of \MicroTEDAClus and its reference implementation are those of Maia et al.~\cite{maia2020evolvclus}, and its updated formulation is that of Galindo~\cite{galindo2021lastmile}. Cardoso~\cite{cardoso2022microtedaclus} had earlier observed that the outlier condition cannot hold for microclusters with two samples, and proposed an intersection condition with a factor. We execute the benchmark models through the \codename{River} library \cite{montiel2021river} or, for \CEDAS, \TEDACloud and \AutoCloud, through implementations that we made from their official code or papers. Our contribution is the reassessment of the formulation, the implementation and the methodology, the methodology that replaces it, the vectorized implementation, the experiments of the improvement and \MicroTEDAClusPlus. The code and the sources of this thesis are public~\cite{carvalho2026microtedaclusreassessment}.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

Our objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}[a)]
\item to examine the formulation of \MicroTEDAClus and its public implementation, and identify their issues and differences;
\item to examine the methodology of the experiments of the original work, and define one that evaluates every model alike and without its flaws;
\item to evaluate \MicroTEDAClus against benchmark models under that methodology;
\item to propose an improved model, and evaluate it under the same methodology against \MicroTEDAClus and the benchmark models.
\end{enumerate}

\Cref{chap:background_relatedwork,chap:methodology_reassessment} address the first three objectives, \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} the last, and \cref{chap:conclusions} concludes the thesis.
