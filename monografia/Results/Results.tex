\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

In this chapter, we place \MicroTEDAClusPlus, the model that we defined in \cref{sec:mi_final_model}, among the benchmark models. We repeat the analysis of the complete model set of \cref{sec:reassessment_results} with \MicroTEDAClusPlus in the place of \MicroTEDAClus: the six other models of that section, whose results are those already reported, and \MicroTEDAClusPlus. We compare the two versions of the model with each other in \cref{sec:mi_main_experiment}.

We apply the statistical analysis of \cref{sec:statistical_significance} to these seven models. Since the best and the worst model of a dataset can change when the set of models changes, the groups of the tables of this chapter are not necessarily those of the corresponding tables of \cref{sec:reassessment_results}. As there, a value in bold is statistically indistinguishable from the best model for that metric and dataset, and a value in red is statistically indistinguishable from the worst. Each table uses the runs that are valid for all of its models, which are 93 of the 94 in \BenchmarkOne, \BenchmarkTwo and \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents, so some of its medians differ in the last digit from those of \cref{sec:mi_main_experiment}.

In the Overall ARI, \cref{tab:plus-vs-benchmarks_ari_overall}, \MicroTEDAClusPlus is indistinguishable from the best model in four of the six datasets and from the worst in none. It is the sole best model in \BenchmarkOne and \BenchmarkTwo, and shares the best group with \TEDACloud in \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents and with \CluStream and \StreamKM in \ChameleonDSFour. In \cref{sec:reassessment_results}, the best model of \BenchmarkOne was \TEDACloud and those of \BenchmarkTwo were \CluStream and \MicroTEDAClus. It is outside the best group in \ChameleonDSThree, where \CluStream and \StreamKM lead, and in \Cassini, where \StreamKM remains the sole best model.

At the penultimate batch, \cref{tab:plus-vs-benchmarks_ari_steadystate}, \MicroTEDAClusPlus is indistinguishable from the best model in the three datasets with concept drift: in \BenchmarkOne and \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents together with \TEDACloud, and in \BenchmarkTwo together with \StreamKM. In the three datasets of static shapes the best group is formed by \CluStream, \StreamKM or both, and \MicroTEDAClusPlus is outside it.

At the fifth batch, \cref{tab:plus-vs-benchmarks_ari_fifth}, \MicroTEDAClusPlus is indistinguishable from the best model in four datasets, and is the sole best model in \ChameleonDSThree and \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents. It is outside the best group in \ChameleonDSFour, led by \CluStream, and in \Cassini, led by \StreamKM. In the maximum ARI drop, \cref{tab:plus-vs-benchmarks_ari_diff}, it is indistinguishable from the best model in \BenchmarkTwo and from the worst in \Cassini, and in neither group in the other four datasets. The smallest drops of this table belong in part to models whose ARI is close to zero throughout the stream, such as \CEDAS in the Chameleon datasets, which cannot drop.

\section{Hyperparameter Variability}
\label{sec:results_parameters}

\input{Results/artifacts/parameters/MicroTEDAClusPlus_parameters.tex}

\Cref{tab:params_microtedaclusplus} gives the dispersion of the hyperparameters selected for \MicroTEDAClusPlus over the 94 runs, in the format of \cref{sec:parameter_variability}. Two observations follow from its comparison with the table of \MicroTEDAClus, \cref{tab:params_microtedaclus}.

The first concerns the variance limit. The median of the limit selected for \MicroTEDAClus spans four orders of magnitude across the datasets, from $\approx 10^{-3}$ in \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents to $\approx 10$ in \ChameleonDSFour, because it is an absolute variance and the datasets have different scales. The median of the relative limit of \MicroTEDAClusPlus spans little more than one and is nearly the same in the three datasets of static shapes, whose coordinates differ in scale by two orders of magnitude. As \cref{sec:parameter_variability} reports for \MicroTEDAClus, its dispersion is larger in the three datasets with concept drift than in the three without it.

The second concerns the new hyperparameter. The noise distance has a low dispersion in every dataset. The decay shows no relation with the concept drift. In the Chameleon datasets every run processes the same stream, as we set in \cref{subsec:mi_runs}, so the dispersion of their rows is that of the hyperparameter search alone.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:results_summary}

Among the seven models and under the protocol of the main experiment, \MicroTEDAClusPlus stands as follows.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clustering quality.} It is indistinguishable from the best model in four of the six datasets in the Overall ARI and in three in the Steady-State ARI, and from the worst in none on either metric. It leads in the datasets with concept drift, where it is the sole best model in the Overall ARI of \BenchmarkOne and \BenchmarkTwo. It does not lead in the static shapes of \ChameleonDSThree and \Cassini, where \CluStream and \StreamKM are better. In the scalability streams it has the highest median ARI at the penultimate batch from two dimensions on, tied with \CEDAS from four.
    \item \textbf{Execution time.} It is indistinguishable from the fastest model in the fitting and prediction stages of the six datasets, and the fastest at every dimension of the scalability streams.
    \item \textbf{Memory and microclusters.} It is not among the most economical models. \CEDAS and \TEDACloud use less memory and keep fewer microclusters, and \MicroTEDAClusPlus is in the intermediate range, well below \CluStream.
\end{itemize}

In \cref{sec:reassessment_results}, we found \MicroTEDAClus to be outperformed by these benchmark models in several metrics: it was among the worst models in the Overall ARI of \BenchmarkOne and among the slowest in the fitting stage. \MicroTEDAClusPlus is the best model of that dataset and the fastest of the seven. The weakness that remains is the clustering of static shapes, which we relate in \cref{sec:mi_selection} to the configurations that the hyperparameter search selects.
