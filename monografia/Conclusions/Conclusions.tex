\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

In this work, we set out to reassess \MicroTEDAClus, an evolving clustering algorithm for data streams built on the eccentricity and typicality of the TEDA framework, and to propose an improved model from that reassessment. We then modernized the experiment and evaluated the improved model on real data.

\section{Final Remarks}
\label{sec:final_remarks}

The formulation of \MicroTEDAClus has issues of its own, an order-dependent density, an outlier condition that cannot hold for microclusters with two samples and an unjustified scaling by the dimension (\cref{subsubsec:order_dependent_characteristics,subsubsec:incorrect_condition_logic,subsubsec:dimensionality_penalty}), and the implementation that produced the published results departs from it (\cref{sec:model_implementation}). The methodology of the original experiments leaks information about the streams, makes most batches trivial to cluster and evaluates each batch after learning it (\cref{chap:methodology_reassessment}). Under a methodology without these flaws, the original model is not on par with the benchmark models of the original work, and it does not adapt better than them to concept drift (\cref{sec:disc_claims}).

\MicroTEDAClusPlus, which we defined by controlled experiments on separate development and validation runs, differs from \MicroTEDAClus in the five points of \cref{tab:mi_final_differences}. On the synthetic datasets it improves on the original model in clustering quality, execution time and memory, no benchmark model is better than it in the datasets with concept drift, and it is the fastest model of the main experiment (\cref{sec:mi_main_experiment}). The modernized experiment, which runs in a single environment with benchmark models from maintained libraries, search ranges by rule and the cost measured with each task alone, changes that position. \MicroTEDAClusPlus is not worse than any benchmark model in the Overall ARI of \BenchmarkOne and \Cassini nor in any scalability stream, but most of them are better than it in \BenchmarkTwo, at the penultimate batch of \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents and in \ChameleonDSThree, and it no longer learns fastest. The protocol itself raises its results in several datasets (\cref{chap:modernization}).

On real datasets, with the same rules for the search ranges and without its noise label, since these datasets have no noise class, \MicroTEDAClusPlus is better than every benchmark model in \Shuttle and worse than most of them in four datasets. Its summary exceeds the limit in most runs of \Covertype, which leaves it out of that dataset (\cref{tab:real_quality,tab:real_failures}). It predicts at least as fast as every benchmark model, learns more slowly than the fastest of them, and reaches the largest peak of memory in three datasets (\cref{tab:real_cost}).

We met the objectives of \cref{sec:objectives}. Our reassessment identified the issues of the formulation, of the implementation and of the methodology, and we replaced the latter by a methodology that evaluates every model alike. The improved model overcomes most weaknesses of the original one on synthetic data, and keeps part of its standing in the modernized experiment. On real data, the evaluation places it ahead of the benchmark models in one dataset and behind most of them in four, within the limits stated in \cref{sec:disc_limits}.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

The results point to the following directions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter selection.} The search selects configurations worse than those its space holds (\cref{sec:mi_selection}), and its outcome varies with its seed (\cref{sec:disc_protocol}). An objective that observes a longer segment of the stream, more trials, or repeated searches would reduce these effects, applied to every model of a comparison alike.
    \item \textbf{One model for both kinds of data.} The noise label helps on data with noise, and on data whose classes cover every sample we run the model without it (\cref{sec:disc_data}). Searching whether to label noise, as one more hyperparameter, would let a single model serve both.
    \item \textbf{The real streams with concept drift.} They are where the model stays behind several benchmark models, and along \InsectsAbrupt it falls below all of them after the first change point (\cref{tab:real_quality,fig:real_insects_abrupt}); they are the main case to understand.
    \item \textbf{Size of the summary.} The summary of the model exceeds the limit in most runs of \Covertype and in some of \Pendigits, and its peak of memory is the largest of the models compared in three real datasets (\cref{tab:real_failures,tab:real_cost}). A bound on the number of microclusters that the model enforces itself would make its cost predictable.
    \item \textbf{An improvement effort on real data.} The improvement of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} rests on synthetic data only. An effort of the same kind on real data, with development runs of its own, could address the weaknesses above.
    \item \textbf{Explore its strengths.} It predicts faster than every benchmark model in every real dataset compared, or as fast as one of them (\cref{tab:real_cost}), which suits applications in which labels must be returned quickly.
\end{itemize}
