\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

In this work, we set out to reassess \MicroTEDAClus, an evolving clustering algorithm for data streams built on the TEDA framework, and to propose an improved model from that reassessment.

\section{Final Remarks}
\label{sec:final_remarks}

The formulation of \MicroTEDAClus has issues of its own, an order-dependent density, an outlier condition that cannot hold for microclusters with two samples and an unjustified scaling by the dimension (\cref{subsubsec:order_dependent_characteristics,subsubsec:incorrect_condition_logic,subsubsec:dimensionality_penalty}), and the implementation that produced the published results departs from it (\cref{sec:model_implementation}). The methodology of the original experiments leaks information about the streams, makes most batches trivial to cluster and evaluates each batch after learning it, and its benchmark models ran through layers of interface that inflated their times (\cref{chap:methodology_reassessment}). Under a methodology that removes these conditions for every model alike, the original model is not on par with the benchmark models of the original work, and it does not adapt better than them to concept drift: at the penultimate batch it is better than none of the established benchmark models in any dataset, and in the scalability streams of high dimension its quality collapses, which we trace to the dimensionality penalty (\cref{sec:reassessment_results}).

\MicroTEDAClusPlus, which we defined by controlled experiments on separate development and validation runs, differs from \MicroTEDAClus in the five points of \cref{tab:mi_final_differences}. It is better than \MicroTEDAClus in the Overall ARI of five of the six datasets and in the Steady-State ARI of all six, faster in both stages and smaller in memory; no benchmark model is better than it in the streams with concept drift, and it is the fastest model of the main experiment (\cref{subsec:mi_main_summary}). Its weakness is the static shapes, where \CluStream and \StreamKM remain better.

We thus met the objectives of \cref{sec:objectives}, within the limits that follow.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}
\label{sec:limits}

Four limits qualify these results.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Streams that repeat.} The Chameleon datasets are fixed files (\cref{subsec:mi_runs}). Their runs repeat the search and not the stream, so their dispersion is that of the search, and their validation confirms the outcome of the search more than that of the model.
    \item \textbf{The search as a source of variation.} Every result measures a model as its protocol tunes it. Two searches of a single algorithm give medians that differ without significance (\cref{tab:mi_refinements_tuning_noise}): differences of that size are not effects of a model, and only the tests over many runs separate the effects from this variation.
    \item \textbf{Cost.} The execution time and the memory measure implementations and not only algorithms, as \cref{subsec:mi_main_cost} notes and as the reimplementation of \cref{sec:c_implementation} shows for a single algorithm, and the models of a comparison are implemented in different libraries.
    \item \textbf{Datasets.} The datasets are streams and shapes of two dimensions and scalability streams of up to sixty-four, all synthetic. Our conclusions extend to data of that kind and not beyond.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter selection.} The search selects configurations worse than those its space holds (\cref{sec:mi_selection}), and its outcome varies with its seed (\cref{tab:mi_refinements_tuning_noise}). An objective that observes a longer segment of the stream, more trials or repeated searches could reduce these effects, applied to every model of a comparison alike.
    \item \textbf{The static shapes.} They are where the model stays behind \CluStream and \StreamKM (\cref{tab:mi_comparison_quality}), and where the probe of \cref{sec:mi_selection} finds far better configurations than the search selects; they are the main case to understand.
    \item \textbf{The noise label.} It improved the model on streams that carry noise (\cref{sec:mi_improvements}). Searching whether to label noise, as one more hyperparameter, would let a single model serve data whose classes cover every sample.
    \item \textbf{Explore its strengths.} It predicts faster than every benchmark model of the main experiment (\cref{tab:mi_comparison_cost}), which suits applications in which labels must be returned quickly.
\end{itemize}
