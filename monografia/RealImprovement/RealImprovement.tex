\chapter{Model Improvement on Real Data}
\label{chap:real_improvement}

\MicroTEDAClusPlus is worse than most benchmark models in six of the eight real datasets of \cref{chap:real_data} (\cref{tab:real_quality}). In this chapter, we repeat on these datasets the effort of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}: we examine what the model does on them, evaluate changes to it, keep three, and confirm them on new runs and on datasets that took no part in their choice. The result is the final version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus, which we then compare with the benchmark models by the protocol of \cref{chap:real_data}, in quality and in cost. Where the two versions appear together, we call the model of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} ``\MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}'', and \modelname{MicroTEDAClus+ (Ch.~\ref{chap:ModelImprovement})} in the tables.

We diagnose the model and develop the changes on six of the eight real datasets, and leave \KDDCup and \Covertype out of both: they are the two longest streams (\cref{tab:real_datasets}), too costly for the many evaluations of the development, and in \Covertype every model scores lower than in any other dataset (\cref{tab:real_quality}), which leaves little to separate the candidates. The two return in the confirmation, where they show whether the changes hold on data that took no part in their choice.

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:ri_diagnosis}

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/diagnosis.tex}

\Cref{tab:ri_diagnosis} gives the state of the \MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} at every batch of the evaluation segments of the runs of \cref{chap:real_data}, on the six datasets of the development. In the INSECTS datasets and in \Pendigits, most of its microclusters never reach three samples, so few of them mature and fewer are active: the activation, the macroclusters and the prediction rest on a small share of the microclusters. In \GasDrift and \Shuttle, most microclusters mature. In \InsectsAbrupt the model labels nearly half of the samples as noise, and in \InsectsAbrupt and \InsectsReoccurring it predicts fewer clusters than there are classes, a single one in \InsectsReoccurring.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:ri_design}

The design is that of \cref{sec:mi_design}, with the changes that this section describes. We tune and evaluate every candidate by the protocol of \cref{sec:real_protocol}.

\subsection{Runs}
\label{subsec:ri_runs}

The runs of \cref{chap:real_data} are the runs 0 to 31. We develop the changes on the 32 runs that follow them, and every candidate uses, in each task, the seeds of the previous \MicroTEDAClusPlus version in that task, so that two candidates differ only by their changes. We confirm the kept changes on the runs 0 to 31 of the eight datasets, on which no candidate ran during the development. The INSECTS datasets and \Covertype keep the order of their files (\cref{subsec:real_order}), so every run of them processes the same stream, and their runs differ only in the seeds of the search.

\subsection{Decision Rule}
\label{subsec:ri_decision_rule}

We compare a candidate with its control as in \cref{subsec:mi_decision_rule}, on the Overall ARI of the six datasets and on the Steady-State ARI of the four whose penultimate batch does not hold a single class (\cref{tab:real_single_class}). Unlike in \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}, a candidate does not need to improve its control to be kept. We keep a candidate if

\begin{itemize}
    \item it improves its control;
    \item it is indistinguishable from its control, its change is analytically preferred, and its summary is not clearly larger;
    \item it is mixed, its gains outnumber its losses, and none of its losses, which we examine before the decision, comes from a defect of the change.
\end{itemize}

A kept candidate becomes the control of the candidates that follow it. As the cost of a candidate we report the size of its summary; we did not measure time in the development, since its tasks ran in parallel.

\section{Kept Changes}
\label{sec:ri_kept}

We keep three changes, each applied to the model that results from the previous ones. \Cref{tab:ri_chain} compares every step of the chain with the step before it.

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/chain.tex}

\subsection{Variance Limit}
\label{subsec:ri_low_variance}

\variant{low-variance} searches the variance limit from a tenth of the lower end of the range of the relative variance (\cref{tab:real_rules}). With the range of \cref{chap:real_data}, the selected limit lies at the lower end of its range in most runs of \InsectsAbrupt, \InsectsIncremental and \Pendigits (\cref{tab:ri_variance}), so the search would select smaller limits if it could. With the extended range, it lies at the lower end in no run.

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/variance.tex}

The change widens the search of \MicroTEDAClusPlus and of no other model. The ranges of \cref{chap:real_data} come from the tuning segment by rule, so a wider range does not suit one scale only. In that chapter, we extended the lower end of the distances of the benchmark models to a tenth of the median distance from a sample to its nearest neighbour (\cref{tab:real_rules}), and left the lower end of the relative variance at the square of that distance.

\variant{low-variance} improves \MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} in the Overall ARI of the three INSECTS datasets and of \Pendigits, and in the Steady-State ARI of \Pendigits (\cref{tab:ri_chain}). Its steady-state summary is larger where it improves.

\subsection{Noise Label}
\label{subsec:ri_no_noise}

\variant{no-noise} removes the noise label of \MicroTEDAClusPlus (\cref{eq:mi_final_prediction}): the model assigns every sample to the macrocluster of its nearest active microcluster, and the noise distance is no longer a hyperparameter. The classes of the real datasets cover every sample (\cref{sec:real_datasets}), so a sample labelled as noise is always an error of the partition, and the previous version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus labels nearly half of the samples of \InsectsAbrupt as noise (\cref{tab:ri_diagnosis}).

\variant{low-variance + no-noise} improves \variant{low-variance} in the Overall ARI of \InsectsReoccurring, and loses in no dataset (\cref{tab:ri_chain}). It fails in more runs, and its summary is larger in \InsectsReoccurring.

\subsection{Link Factor}
\label{subsec:ri_link}

\variant{link-factor} is the candidate of \cref{subsec:mi_improvement_candidates} that searches the factor $\ell$ of the intersection condition (\cref{eq:microcluster_intersection_condition}) in $[0.5, 4]$. Applied to \variant{low-variance}, it improves it with a single gain, while its median Overall ARI is lower in four of the six datasets (\cref{tab:ri_candidates}). Since we kept \variant{no-noise} against the same control, we evaluate the two changes together: we keep \variant{link-factor} if we keep \variant{low-variance + no-noise + link-factor} against \variant{low-variance + no-noise}.

The combination is mixed (\cref{tab:ri_chain}): it gains in the Overall ARI of \GasDrift and \Pendigits and in the Steady-State ARI of \GasDrift, and loses in the Overall ARI of \InsectsIncremental and \InsectsReoccurring. The search selects factors close to the fixed factor of two in the INSECTS datasets and different factors in the others (\cref{tab:ri_link}).

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/link.tex}

Where the selected factor is that of the control, the model is nearly the control, found by a search with one more hyperparameter and the same number of trials, which we already found to cost quality in \cref{subsec:mi_m_function}. We attribute the losses to the search and not to the change, and the gains outnumber them, so we keep the combination.

\section{Rejected Candidates}
\label{sec:ri_rejected}

\Cref{tab:ri_candidates} gives the outcome of every other candidate against its control. Its last rows are \variant{link-factor} applied alone to \variant{low-variance}, before the combination test of \cref{subsec:ri_link}, and \variant{wide-link} against its control and against the last step of the chain.

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/candidates.tex}

\paragraph{Sensitivity function.} Two candidates come from \cref{subsec:mi_m_function}, \variant{m-constant} and \variant{m-reparam}, and three more change the function $m$ of \cref{eq:m_function}: \variant{m-fixed} fixes $m$ at three from the third sample on, \variant{m-sigmoid} searches both constants of the sigmoid, and \variant{m-rate} keeps the asymptote of three and searches how fast $m$ approaches it. All five regress, and we retain the original function, as in \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}.

\paragraph{Geometry.} \variant{diagonal-variance} gives a microcluster one variance per feature, and \variant{covariance} is the candidate of \cref{subsec:mi_covariance} with the directional link. Both regress. We evaluated \variant{covariance} first on eight runs of \InsectsIncremental, \Pendigits and \Shuttle, three datasets with fewer features than \GasDrift, because each of its microclusters carries a matrix of the square of the dimension and a full development would have taken several hours, most of them in \GasDrift. Its probe already lost in two cells, and \variant{diagonal-variance}, which also standardizes the distance feature by feature, had regressed in five, so we did not run the full development. We retain the scalar variance.

\paragraph{Density.} \variant{volume-density} is the density of \cref{sec:mi_corrections}, and \variant{log-density} is the logarithm of the number of samples per volume of the microcluster. \variant{volume-density} is mixed with one gain and one loss, which the rule does not keep, and \variant{log-density} regresses. We did not run \variant{diagonal-variance + log-density}, since both of its changes regress alone and the pair would have to undo both regressions to be kept.

\paragraph{Activation and pruning.} Two candidates of \cref{subsec:mi_improvement_candidates}, \variant{no-activation} and \variant{recency}, are mixed, with more losses than gains. \variant{mature-mean}, the correction of \cref{sec:mi_corrections}, is indistinguishable from its control and analytically preferred, but its summary is larger in one dataset, so we do not keep it.

\paragraph{Membership.} \variant{norm-typicality} and \variant{local-weights} are the memberships of \cref{sec:mi_corrections}. Both regress, and we retain the nearest membership.

\paragraph{Dimensionality penalty.} \variant{dimensionality-penalty} restores the factor $2/d$ that we removed from \MicroTEDAClusPlus in \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}. It is mixed, with more losses than gains, and its summary is smaller than that of its control in every dataset.

\paragraph{Departures from the rule.} We did not keep two candidates that the rule would keep. \variant{chi-threshold}, the candidate of \cref{subsec:mi_improvement_candidates} that tests the outliers against the dispersion of a Gaussian cluster in $d$ dimensions, improves \variant{low-variance} with a single gain while its median Overall ARI is lower in five of the six datasets (\cref{tab:ri_candidates}). \variant{wide-link}, the link factor searched in $[0.05, 4]$ on a logarithmic scale, is mixed against its control, \variant{low-variance + no-noise}, and in the planned direct comparison it improves \variant{low-variance + no-noise + link-factor} with two gains, while its median Overall ARI is lower in five of the six datasets. In both cases we judged a single favourable dataset to weigh less than a consistent lean in the others. We also compared the medians of the two link factors with those of the benchmark models in \cref{tab:real_quality}, outside the rule, and that comparison favoured the narrower range. The link factor itself, whose median Overall ARI was also lower in most datasets, was kept by the combination test of \cref{subsec:ri_link}; before that test, what distinguished it from \variant{chi-threshold} was only the size of its gain, which the rule does not consider.

\section{Confirmation}
\label{sec:ri_confirmation}

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/confirmation.tex}

\Cref{tab:ri_confirmation} repeats the chain on the runs 0 to 31 of the eight datasets. \variant{low-variance} improves the previous \MicroTEDAClusPlus version in the Overall ARI of the three INSECTS datasets and of \Pendigits and in the Steady-State ARI of \Pendigits, the same gains as in the development (\cref{tab:ri_chain}), and its summary is again larger where it improves. The removal of the noise label improves \variant{low-variance} in \InsectsReoccurring, as in the development. The link factor gains in \Pendigits and loses in \InsectsReoccurring, as in the development, and also gains in \Covertype; its gain in \GasDrift and its loss in \InsectsIncremental are not significant on these runs.

No step loses where the development found it to gain, and no step loses in the two datasets that took no part in the development. In \KDDCup no step changes the model significantly. In \Covertype the steps fail in many runs, all by the limit on the summary of \cref{sec:real_protocol} (\cref{tab:ri_confirmation}).

\section{The Final Version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus}
\label{sec:ri_final}

The final version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus differs from the version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} in the three points of \cref{tab:ri_differences}. Its hyperparameters are the relative variance limit $r$, the decay and the link factor $\ell$.

We tune and evaluate the final version by the protocol of \cref{chap:real_data}, as every model of that chapter, on the runs 0 to 31 and 64 to 125, which make the 94 runs of \cref{subsec:multi_run_statistics} and leave out the development runs. We evaluate the benchmark models on the same runs, adding the runs 64 to 125 to those of \cref{chap:real_data}, and measure the time and memory of every model by the procedure of \cref{sec:mod_measurements} on the runs 0 to 31. Its search uses seeds of its own, as that of every model of \cref{chap:real_data}, where the confirmation used those of \MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} to pair the steps, so its medians differ somewhat from those of the last step of \cref{tab:ri_confirmation}.

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/partitions.tex}

\Cref{tab:ri_partitions} compares the partitions that the two versions predict. The final version labels no sample as noise, and in every dataset but \KDDCup it predicts more clusters than there are classes, several times more in \InsectsAbrupt and \Covertype. Its homogeneity is higher than that of the version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} in five datasets and its completeness is lower in most: the final version splits the classes over more clusters, where the version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} merged them or labelled their samples as noise. Where the version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} predicts a single cluster, in \InsectsReoccurring, \KDDCup and \Covertype, its completeness is one by construction.

\section{Comparison with the Benchmark Models}
\label{sec:ri_comparison}

\subsection{Clustering Quality}
\label{subsec:ri_quality}

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/quality.tex}
\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/failures.tex}

The final version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus is better than every benchmark model in the Overall ARI of \Shuttle and not worse than any in \Pendigits, where it is better than \DenStream (\cref{tab:ri_quality}). In \GasDrift it is better than two of them and worse than \MiniBatchKMeans. It remains worse than most benchmark models in \InsectsIncremental, \KDDCup and \Covertype, and worse than \DenStream and \BICO in \InsectsAbrupt and \InsectsReoccurring. In \KDDCup its median is about that of the \MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement}, which in \cref{chap:real_data} was worse than every benchmark model in four datasets, \Pendigits among them, and better than most only in \Shuttle (\cref{tab:real_quality}). At the penultimate batch, the final version is not worse than any benchmark model in \GasDrift, \Pendigits and \Shuttle, and worse than three in \InsectsIncremental and \KDDCup. It fails in a few runs of four datasets and in about a quarter of the runs of \Covertype, all by the limit on the summary (\cref{tab:ri_failures}).

The final version starts slowly. Over the first fifty batches of the streams that keep their order (\cref{fig:ri_slow_start}), its ARI is zero over the first few hundred samples, as is that of \DenStream in the INSECTS datasets. It then stays below the benchmark models until about 2,600 samples in \InsectsAbrupt and about 1,500 in \InsectsIncremental, while in \InsectsReoccurring and \Covertype it reaches their level within about a thousand samples.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{subsec:ri_cost}

\input{RealImprovement/artifacts/cost.tex}

The final version is faster than every benchmark model in the prediction stage of six datasets (\cref{tab:ri_cost}). In the learning stage it is slower than \MiniBatchKMeans in every dataset, slower than \BICO in all but \GasDrift, and slower than every benchmark model in \Shuttle, while it is faster than \DBSTREAM in five datasets. The memory it holds at the end of the stream is larger than that of \MiniBatchKMeans in every dataset and than that of \DenStream in five, and its peak during the evaluation is larger than those of every benchmark model in five datasets.

\subsection{Remaining Limits}
\label{subsec:ri_limits}

Three limits of the final version remain. It splits the classes of the INSECTS datasets over many clusters (\cref{tab:ri_partitions}), while its search selects there a link factor close to the fixed factor of the version of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} (\cref{tab:ri_link}), although a larger factor would join more microclusters. It starts slowly (\cref{fig:ri_slow_start}). And its summary exceeds the limit in some runs of \Covertype (\cref{tab:ri_failures}), and the peak of its memory is the largest of the models compared in most datasets (\cref{tab:ri_cost}).

We evaluated the final version on the real datasets only, and not on the synthetic datasets of \cref{chap:modernization}. Its removal of the noise label suits data whose classes cover every sample, while on the synthetic datasets with noise we found the noise label to improve the model (\cref{sec:mi_improvements}).

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:ri_summary}

On the real datasets of \cref{chap:real_data}, the \MicroTEDAClusPlus of \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} is worse than most benchmark models in most datasets. Most of its microclusters never mature in the INSECTS datasets and in \Pendigits, and it labels as noise samples of classes that cover the whole stream (\cref{tab:ri_diagnosis}). Of the candidates that we evaluated on six of the datasets, we kept three: the variance limit searched one decade lower, the removal of the noise label and a searched link factor (\cref{tab:ri_chain}). The alternatives to the function $m$, to the isotropic variance, to the density, to the activation and to the membership did not improve the model, as in \cref{chap:ModelImprovement} (\cref{tab:ri_candidates}). The chain held on new runs and on the two datasets left out of the development (\cref{tab:ri_confirmation}).

Tuned and evaluated as every model of \cref{chap:real_data}, over 94 runs, the final version of \MicroTEDAClusPlus is better than every benchmark model in \Shuttle and not worse than any in \Pendigits, but it remains worse than most of them in three datasets (\cref{tab:ri_quality}). It starts slowly and splits the classes of the INSECTS datasets over many clusters (\cref{fig:ri_slow_start,tab:ri_partitions}). It predicts faster than every benchmark model in most datasets, but learns more slowly than the fastest of them and reaches the largest peak of memory in most datasets (\cref{tab:ri_cost}).
