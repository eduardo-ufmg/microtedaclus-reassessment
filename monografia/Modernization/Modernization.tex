\chapter{Experiment Modernization}
\label{chap:modernization}

The main experiment of the previous chapters runs part of its generators in \codename{R} and \codename{Java}, as \cref{chap:methodology_reassessment} describes, and its protocol carries the constraints of that environment. Its hyperparameter search penalizes the execution time and stops at a time limit, and its datasets have at most 10000 samples, because some of the models are too slow for an unrestricted search, as seen in \cref{subsec:hyperparameter_tuning}. We set the ranges of that search by hand, as values that seemed reasonable after a brief survey of the literature \cite{cao2006density,aggarwal2003framework,hyde2017fully,ackermann2012streamkmpp,bezerra2016tedacloud,bezerra2020autocloud}. In \cref{sec:mi_selection}, we found that the objective of the search observes too short a segment of the stream to recognize the configurations that are best in the long run, and that the search is itself a source of variation.

In this chapter, we replace the environment, the benchmark models and the hyperparameter selection, and repeat the main experiment under them. We evaluate \MicroTEDAClusPlus as \cref{sec:mi_final_model} defines it, without changes.

\section{Environment}
\label{sec:mod_environment}

We write the experiment in \codename{Python} only. Its numerical parts run in compiled code through the libraries that provide them, and no other runtime is involved. \MicroTEDAClusPlus keeps the implementation of \cref{sec:mi_final_model}, and we use every model through one interface: it predicts the labels of a batch, learns the batch, and reports the number of structures of its summary of the stream, which generalizes the microcluster count of \cref{sec:evaluation_metrics}. We label as noise a sample that a model predicts before it has any cluster.

\subsection{Stream Generators}
\label{subsec:mod_generators}

Four generators of \cref{sec:datasets} ran in \codename{R}: that of \Cassini and those of \BenchmarkOne, \BenchmarkTwo and \RandomRBFGeneratorEvents, the last from MOA \cite{bifet2010moa} through \codename{streamMOA} \cite{streamMOA2024}. We port them to \codename{Python}, following their reference implementations step by step. The Chameleon datasets remain the files of \cref{sec:datasets}, and the scalability streams were already generated in \codename{Python}. A stream is determined by its dataset and its run, which seed its generator.

We check each port against the streams that \codename{R} generated for the previous experiment through the ARI of three models with fixed hyperparameters, \cref{tab:mod_generators_behaviour}, which we compare by the Mann-Whitney test with the Holm correction and the threshold on Cliff's delta of \cref{sec:statistical_significance}. No test finds a difference.

\subsection{Length and Order of the Streams}
\label{subsec:mod_streams}

The generated streams are five times longer than those of the previous experiment, and so are their tuning segments, in answer to \cref{sec:mi_selection}. The Chameleon files keep their size. We shuffle the streams without concept drift, which are those of \Cassini, of the Chameleon datasets and of the scalability datasets, anew in every run. Each run of a Chameleon dataset thus has a stream of its own, where in the previous experiment all its runs processed one same stream, \cref{subsec:mi_selection_consequences}. The concept drift streams keep their order, which carries the drift.

\section{Benchmark Models}
\label{sec:mod_models}

We choose the benchmark models by three criteria:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item They are established in the literature of stream clustering;
    \item Their implementations are maintained libraries that the experiment imports instead of copying;
    \item They are fast enough to be tuned without a time limit.
\end{enumerate}

We retain five models: \DenStream \cite{cao2006density} and \DBSTREAM \cite{hahsler2016dbstream}, from \codename{River} \cite{montiel2021river}; \BIRCH \cite{zhang1996birch} and \MiniBatchKMeans \cite{sculley2010webscale}, from \codename{scikit-learn} \cite{pedregosa2011scikit}; and \BICO \cite{fichtenberger2013bico}, from the \codename{bico} package. They summarize the stream by density-based microclusters that fade, \DenStream and \DBSTREAM; by a tree of cluster features, \BIRCH; by a coreset, \BICO; and by centroids, \MiniBatchKMeans. This set replaces that of \cref{chap:results}.

\Cref{tab:mod_hyperparameters} lists the hyperparameters that the search selects for each model. We search a parameter if it is part of the clustering method, and keep the default of its library if it only sets a unit of time or the shape of a data structure. \DenStream and \DBSTREAM forget at a rate that we search as its half-life, in samples. We search the outlier threshold $\beta\mu$ of \DenStream, which its formulation requires to exceed one, as its position between one and $\mu$.

\section{Hyperparameter Selection}
\label{sec:mod_selection}

The search remains the Bayesian optimization of \cref{subsec:hyperparameter_tuning} on the first 20\% of each stream, and we evaluate the selected model prequentially on the rest. Three things change: where the ranges come from, what the objective measures, and what bounds the search.

\subsection{Ranges by Rule}
\label{subsec:mod_ranges}

Each hyperparameter belongs to a category, given in \cref{tab:mod_hyperparameters}, and each category has one rule that computes its range from the tuning segment, given in \cref{tab:mod_rules}. A rule uses only what a deployment of the model also has before the stream continues: the samples of the segment and their labels. Below the median distance from a sample to its nearest neighbour, a structure of that radius holds a single sample, and above the median distance between two samples it holds half of the segment, so the distances of the rules span the scales at which a structure can summarize the data. The longest horizon is the length of the segment, the longest that the segment can tell apart from not forgetting at all. The rules replace ranges set by hand, which \cref{tab:mi_search_range_selection} showed to depend on the scale of the data. \Cref{tab:mod_ranges} gives the ranges that they produce in each dataset.

\subsection{Objective and Search}
\label{subsec:mod_objective}

The objective is the median of the batch ARI over the second half of the tuning segment. It is the quantity that the Overall ARI reports, computed after the configuration has had the first half of the segment to form its summary, which addresses the slow maturation shown in \cref{tab:mi_selection_maturation}. It has no term of execution time.

Every model receives 100 trials, without a time limit. The search of a task is seeded by its dataset, its model and its run, and its objective involves no measurement of time, so the selection depends neither on the speed of the machine nor on its load. To measure what the change of protocol does, we also tune \MicroTEDAClusPlus by the previous protocol of \cref{subsec:hyperparameter_tuning}, with its previous ranges, on the same streams.

\section{Execution and Measurements}
\label{sec:mod_measurements}

A task is a dataset, a model and a run, and the experiment executes the tasks of 94 runs, 13 datasets and the six models in parallel processes, which give the quality of the models and the size of their summaries. Parallel execution disturbs the execution time, under which the main experiment of the previous chapters timed its tasks. We therefore measure time in a separate pass, by the procedure of \cref{subsec:mi_optimization_time}: we execute the tasks of the first ten runs again, one at a time, with their selected hyperparameters. A new model of the task first processes the first three batches of the evaluation segment and is discarded, so that what is created on first use is in place, and we then time another new model over the evaluation segment once. A single execution replaces the minimum of three, since the three executions of a task agree closely in \cref{tab:mi_validation_serial_cost}.

We measure memory in another pass, over the tasks of the same ten runs, by tracking every allocation of the process with \codename{memray} \cite{memray}, which counts the memory allocated by compiled code as well as that allocated by \codename{Python}. We keep two quantities: the memory still allocated when the stream ends, which is what the model holds, and the peak during the evaluation, which adds the working memory of its steps. Each task runs in a new process, so that it inherits nothing that an earlier task left allocated. A model of the task first processes the whole evaluation segment without tracking, so that what is created on first use is in place however late it is created, and we then track new models of the task three times and keep the smallest values. These quantities are not comparable with the memory reported in the previous chapters, which counted only the \codename{Python} objects of a model.

We compare the results by the analysis of \cref{sec:statistical_significance}, as in \cref{chap:results}: a value in bold is statistically indistinguishable from the best model of its dataset, and a value in red from the worst. When several models share the best or the worst median, the reference is the one of best or worst mean rank over the runs. The comparisons of time and memory rest on ten runs instead of 94, so they separate fewer models. The tables refer to the datasets by the abbreviations of \cref{tab:mi_dataset_abbreviations}, and to \datasetname{SCL-$d$} as S$d$.

\label{subsec:mod_protocols}

\Cref{tab:mod_protocols} compares \MicroTEDAClusPlus under the two protocols on the same streams. \section{Summary}
\label{sec:mod_summary}

The modernized experiment runs in \codename{Python} only, on ported generators validated against those of \codename{R}, \cref{subsec:mod_generators}, with five benchmark models imported from maintained libraries. Its hyperparameter selection takes its ranges from the tuning segment by rule and measures no time, so it does not depend on the speed or the load of the machine. Under it, \MicroTEDAClusPlus is indistinguishable from the best model in most datasets, and the protocol itself improves its results, \cref{tab:mod_protocols}.
